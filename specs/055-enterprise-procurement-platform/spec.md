## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - End-to-End Supplier Lifecycle From Vendor Registration Through Qualified, Active Supplier (Priority: P1)

A Vendor Manager registers a new supplier, who progresses through the platform's unified supplier lifecycle: Vendor Management registration and Vendor Master Record creation, Supplier Information Management (SIM) profile enrichment (compliance certificates, financial statements, ESG reports, insurance documents), and the 10-step Supplier Qualification & Onboarding workflow (Registration → Business Verification → Compliance Review → Financial Assessment → Technical Evaluation → Quality Assessment → Security Assessment → Risk Evaluation → Executive Approval → Onboarding), before the supplier becomes an Active, procurement-eligible vendor.

**Why this priority**: This is the foundational data and governance layer every other procurement capability depends on — Strategic Sourcing, RFx, and Purchase Orders all require a qualified, active supplier record to transact against. Without it, no downstream procurement activity has a valid counterparty.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by registering one new supplier, populating their SIM profile (company overview, registration/tax/compliance certificates), and walking that supplier through all 10 Qualification Workflow steps to Onboarding, confirming the Vendor Master Record transitions through the 11-stage Vendor Lifecycle (Registration → Initial Review → Document Verification → Qualification → Approval → Active) with a complete, immutable audit history at each stage.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a new supplier is registered, **When** the Vendor Manager creates the Vendor Master Record, **Then** the system captures Vendor ID, Legal Business Name, Tax/GST/PAN details, Business Type, Primary/Secondary Contacts, Banking Information, Payment Terms, Procurement Category, Risk Rating, Compliance Status, and AI Trust Score as a single centralized profile.
2. **Given** a supplier is undergoing qualification, **When** the workflow reaches Compliance Review and Financial Assessment, **Then** the system requires the supplier's Registration Certificates, Tax Certificates, Compliance Certifications, and Financial Statements to be present in the SIM repository before the workflow can advance.
3. **Given** a supplier has completed Risk Evaluation, **When** the qualification record reaches the Executive Approval step, **Then** the supplier cannot reach Onboarding or Active vendor status without an explicit, recorded executive approval decision.
4. **Given** an Active supplier's compliance certificate approaches its expiration date, **When** the platform's document governance monitors expiry, **Then** it generates a Document Renewal Alert rather than silently allowing the certificate to lapse unnoticed.

### User Story 2 - Budget-Validated Purchase Request to Purchase Order With Multi-Level, Role-Differentiated Approval (Priority: P1)

A Procurement Manager creates a Purchase Request that must pass Budget Validation before entering a configurable, multi-level Approval Workflow (parallel and/or sequential approvals per an Approval Matrix); only after approval does the system generate a Purchase Order, which itself progresses through its own 12-stage lifecycle (Draft → Budget Validation → Approval → PO Generation → Supplier Acceptance → Fulfillment → Partial/Complete Delivery → Invoice Matching → Payment Authorization → Closure → Archival).

**Why this priority**: Budget-gated, role-differentiated approval is the core financial-governance control of the entire platform (constitution Article VII — Layered, Explicit RBAC With Approval Chains). Every purchase order, regardless of type, must pass through this control, making it as foundational as supplier onboarding.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by submitting one Purchase Request against a department budget, confirming it is blocked from advancing until Budget Validation passes, routing it through a configured Approval Matrix with at least two distinct approval roles, and confirming a Purchase Order is only generated after all required approvals are recorded — with the full approval history retained as an immutable audit trail.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a Purchase Request referencing a department budget, **When** it is submitted, **Then** the system performs Budget Validation before allowing the request to proceed to an approval workflow.
2. **Given** a Purchase Request exceeds the requester's approval authority, **When** it is routed through the Approval Matrix, **Then** the system enforces the configured multi-level approval chain (parallel and/or sequential approvals) rather than allowing a single approver to unilaterally approve it.
3. **Given** all required approvals are recorded, **When** the workflow completes, **Then** the system generates a Purchase Order carrying the Request Number, Budget Reference, and full Approval History, and the PO enters Supplier Acceptance.
4. **Given** an approval step is rejected or escalated, **When** the rejection occurs, **Then** the Purchase Request/PO is halted, the requester and relevant teams are notified, and the rejection is captured in the immutable audit trail.

### User Story 3 - Three-Way Match Gate Before Payment Authorization (Priority: P1)

Before any supplier invoice is authorized for payment, the platform's Goods Receipt & Three-Way Matching capability automatically compares the Purchase Order, the recorded Goods Receipt, and the Supplier Invoice across product, quantity, price, tax, delivery, contract, approval, and currency dimensions within a configurable tolerance, and routes any mismatch (quantity variance, pricing variance, duplicate invoice, missing delivery) to human exception handling rather than authorizing payment.

**Why this priority**: This is the platform's principal financial-fraud and internal-control safeguard, explicitly called out in the source's Enterprise Acceptance Criteria as mandatory ("Three-way matching shall be mandatory before payment authorization"). It is P1 because it directly protects against financial loss and is a stated hard gate, not an optional optimization.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by creating one Purchase Order, recording a Goods Receipt against it, submitting a matching Supplier Invoice, and confirming the system automatically performs the three-way match and authorizes payment only when all match dimensions pass tolerance — then repeating with a deliberately mismatched invoice (e.g., quantity variance) and confirming payment is blocked and an exception is raised instead.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a Purchase Order, a recorded Goods Receipt, and a Supplier Invoice all agree on product, quantity, price, tax, delivery, contract, approval, and currency within configured tolerance, **When** the three-way match runs, **Then** the system authorizes the payment request.
2. **Given** a Supplier Invoice shows a quantity or pricing variance beyond configured tolerance versus the Purchase Order and Goods Receipt, **When** the three-way match runs, **Then** the system MUST NOT authorize payment and instead raises a variance exception for human resolution.
3. **Given** a Supplier Invoice duplicates an already-processed invoice, **When** the match runs, **Then** the system flags it as a Duplicate Invoice exception rather than processing payment a second time.
4. **Given** goods are only partially received against a Purchase Order, **When** the corresponding invoice is submitted, **Then** the system matches only against the delivered quantity and does not authorize payment for undelivered goods.

### User Story 4 - Human-Approval Gate on Strategic, Financial & Regulatory AI Procurement Recommendations (Priority: P1)

Throughout the platform, AI generates advisory procurement recommendations (vendor recommendations, sourcing insights, PO prioritization, contract negotiation assistance, spend optimization, and — in the platform's roadmap vision — fully autonomous procurement agents). Regardless of how advanced the AI capability is described in the roadmap, every recommendation that touches a strategic, financial, or regulatory decision (vendor selection, contract terms, budget commitment, payment authorization, compliance determination) is surfaced as an explainable, confidence-scored suggestion that requires explicit human or role-gated approval before it takes effect — the AI never executes such a decision autonomously.

**Why this priority**: This is a direct application of constitution Article II (AI Is Assistive, Never Autonomous) to the chapter's most ambitious content — the "Future Autonomous Procurement Ecosystem" (Section 29) and "Future Procurement AI" roadmap (Section 22). Without this guardrail being a first-class, testable user story, the roadmap's language ("Autonomous Procurement Agents," "Autonomous Purchase Approvals," "AI Vendor Negotiation") risks being implemented as literally self-executing, which the constitution forbids. It is P1 because it is a governance control that must exist before any AI recommendation feature ships, not an optional enhancement.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by triggering one AI-generated recommendation in each of the strategic (vendor recommendation), financial (spend/budget optimization), and regulatory (compliance/risk classification) categories, and confirming that in every case: the recommendation carries an explanation and confidence indicator, no downstream action (vendor selection finalized, budget committed, PO issued, contract bound) occurs automatically, and the recommendation only takes effect after a recorded human approval action tied to an accountable user/role.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the AI Vendor Intelligence or AI Strategic Sourcing capability generates a preferred-vendor recommendation, **When** the recommendation is presented, **Then** it remains advisory-only and the system does not finalize vendor selection until an authorized human explicitly approves it.
2. **Given** the AI Spend Intelligence or AI Procurement Operations Intelligence capability recommends a budget or spend optimization action, **When** the recommendation is generated, **Then** no budget commitment, purchase order, or payment is executed until a human with appropriate financial approval authority accepts it.
3. **Given** the roadmap's "Autonomous Procurement Agents" or "Autonomous Purchase Approvals" capability is enabled in a future phase, **When** it evaluates a strategic, financial, or regulatory decision, **Then** the system enforces a mandatory (not optional/bypassable) human-approval checkpoint before the decision takes effect, consistent with the source's own statement that "human approval shall remain configurable for strategic, financial, and regulatory decisions" being treated as a required, non-bypassable control.
4. **Given** the AI recommendation service is unavailable, **When** a procurement user needs to make a vendor, sourcing, or spend decision, **Then** the platform provides a deterministic non-AI fallback (e.g., manual vendor comparison, rule-based routing) rather than blocking procurement operations.

### User Story 5 - Strategic Sourcing Through Competitive RFQ, RFP & RFI With AI-Assisted Evaluation (Priority: P2)

A Category Manager runs a sourcing event for a procurement category: issuing an RFI to discover and prequalify candidate suppliers, then either an RFQ (price-driven, with pricing templates and bid comparison) or an RFP (scope/technical/commercial evaluation with technical and commercial scoring), evaluating responses against the 10-criteria Evaluation Framework (price, quality, technical capability, innovation, delivery, financial stability, compliance, sustainability, risk profile, strategic alignment), with AI Procurement Evaluation providing automated proposal comparison and bid scoring as decision support before a human makes the final award recommendation.

**Why this priority**: Strategic sourcing and RFx are the primary mechanism for competitive, auditable vendor selection ahead of purchase orders, but the business can still operate (at reduced efficiency) via direct/negotiated purchase orders without it, placing it below the P1 financial-control stories.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by creating one RFQ with at least two invited suppliers, collecting bids before the submission deadline, running AI-assisted bid comparison, and confirming the award requires an explicit approval step and produces an award notification, with the full evaluation trail retained.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a category requires market discovery, **When** an RFI is issued, **Then** the system supports supplier discovery, capability assessment, and vendor prequalification, and the collected information becomes available to a subsequent RFQ/RFP.
2. **Given** an RFQ is created with pricing templates and a submission deadline, **When** invited suppliers submit bids, **Then** the system supports bid comparison and clarification requests before the evaluation workflow proceeds to vendor selection.
3. **Given** an RFP requires technical and commercial evaluation, **When** proposals are submitted, **Then** the system captures separate Technical Scoring and Commercial Scoring before generating a Final Recommendation that requires Executive Approval.
4. **Given** AI Procurement Evaluation has produced an automated proposal comparison and vendor ranking, **When** the sourcing team reviews it, **Then** the AI output is presented as decision support only — the vendor award decision is recorded as a human action, not an automated one.

### User Story 6 - AI Supplier Intelligence Benchmarking Including ESG Compliance as a Scoring Input (Priority: P2)

A Procurement Director reviews AI Supplier Intelligence for a category of suppliers: performance benchmarking across On-Time Delivery, Quality Performance, Procurement Cost, Invoice Accuracy, Contract Compliance, Issue Resolution Time, Innovation Contribution, Customer Satisfaction, ESG Compliance, and Procurement Efficiency — with ESG Compliance tracked as one of the ten continuously monitored performance dimensions, feeding into supplier risk rating, qualification criteria, and preferred-supplier rankings alongside financial and operational factors.

**Why this priority**: Supplier intelligence and benchmarking materially improve sourcing and vendor-management decisions and are explicitly named as a continuous capability, but they operate on top of an already-qualified, already-transacting supplier base (Stories 1–3), so they are sequenced as P2 enhancement rather than foundational infrastructure.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by generating an AI Supplier Intelligence benchmarking report for a set of active suppliers, confirming ESG Compliance appears as a scored dimension alongside the other nine performance metrics, and confirming the resulting Preferred Supplier Ranking and Procurement Health Index reflect that ESG input without any supplier status change occurring automatically from the AI output alone.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a supplier has performance history, **When** AI Supplier Performance Intelligence runs, **Then** it produces scores across all ten monitored dimensions (including ESG Compliance) rather than omitting ESG from the benchmarking output.
2. **Given** a supplier's ESG Compliance score changes materially, **When** the AI Qualification Intelligence or AI Vendor Intelligence recomputes supplier rankings, **Then** the change is reflected in Preferred Vendor Recommendations and Risk Rating, and the rationale (including the ESG input) is explainable to the reviewing user.
3. **Given** executives request procurement intelligence, **When** the Executive AI Insights are generated, **Then** ESG Compliance status is included among the reported Procurement Compliance and Supplier Performance Trends metrics.
4. **Given** a supplier's ESG Compliance score triggers a downgraded risk rating, **When** the system surfaces this to a Vendor Manager, **Then** any consequential action (e.g., suspension, sourcing exclusion) remains a human decision, not an automatic status change driven solely by the AI score.

### User Story 7 - AI-Driven Inventory Procurement Automation Within Configurable Human-Set Rules (Priority: P2)

An Inventory Manager configures automated procurement rules (reorder points, safety stock, min/max stock, supplier lead time) for a set of SKUs. The platform continuously monitors inventory levels, forecasts demand using historical sales, seasonal trends, and AI predictions, and — within the configured rules — automatically generates Purchase Requests/Purchase Orders for reorder, while low-stock and overstock conditions raise alerts for review rather than silently over- or under-buying.

**Why this priority**: Inventory procurement automation reduces manual replenishment effort and stockout risk, but it operates within — and is bounded by — the same budget-validation and approval controls established in Story 2, and the business can run on manual purchase requests without it, making it P2.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by configuring reorder rules for one SKU, simulating inventory dropping below its configured Reorder Point, and confirming the platform automatically generates a Purchase Request/Purchase Order (which still passes through Budget Validation and Approval per Story 2) rather than bypassing procurement governance, while a simulated overstock condition raises an Overstock Prevention alert instead of an automatic order.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a SKU's Available Inventory falls to its configured Reorder Point, **When** the automated procurement rule evaluates, **Then** the system generates a Purchase Request/Purchase Order sized per the configured rule, which still enters the standard Budget Validation and Approval Workflow.
2. **Given** demand forecasting inputs (historical sales, seasonal trends, AI predictions) indicate an upcoming demand spike, **When** the forecast updates, **Then** the system adjusts its procurement recommendation and surfaces the updated forecast to the Inventory Manager.
3. **Given** projected inventory would exceed configured maximum stock levels, **When** the automation evaluates a potential reorder, **Then** the system applies Overstock Prevention and raises an alert rather than placing an unnecessary order.
4. **Given** an Emergency Procurement condition is detected (e.g., imminent stockout with no standard replenishment in time), **When** the automation triggers Emergency Procurement, **Then** the resulting request is flagged as emergency but still routes through an approval step appropriate to its expedited nature rather than bypassing approval entirely.

### User Story 8 - Executive Procurement Health, Spend Analytics & AI Intelligence Dashboard (Priority: P3)

An executive (CPO, CFO, or CEO) opens the Executive Procurement Dashboard to review the Procurement Health Index, Total Spend, Category/Vendor/Department Spend breakdowns, Budget Utilization, Cost Savings, Supplier Performance, Contract Status, Procurement Risks, and AI-generated forecasts and strategic recommendations — all explainable, auditable, and clearly separated between reported facts and AI-generated advisory insight.

**Why this priority**: Executive analytics consolidate and report on data produced by the other stories rather than generating new procurement transactions themselves, so while valuable for governance and decision-making, it is correctly sequenced last (P3) — it depends on Stories 1–7 already producing data to analyze.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by generating the Executive Procurement Dashboard after at least one completed procurement cycle (supplier onboarded, PO issued and closed, three-way match completed) and confirming Total Spend, Procurement Health Index, Supplier Performance, and at least one AI-generated forecast render correctly, with AI-generated content visibly distinguished from factual reporting.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** completed procurement transactions exist across multiple categories and vendors, **When** an executive opens the dashboard, **Then** Spend Analytics (Total Procurement Spend, Category Spend, Vendor Spend, Department Spend, Budget Utilization, Cost Savings, Spend Under Management) render as consolidated, accurate figures.
2. **Given** AI Spend Intelligence generates a spend forecast or savings opportunity, **When** it is displayed on the dashboard, **Then** it is presented as an explainable, auditable AI insight distinct from historical/actual figures, consistent with constitution Article II.
3. **Given** an executive wants to drill into a specific category's procurement risk, **When** they select it, **Then** the system surfaces the relevant Procurement Risk Analytics and any associated AI Executive Insights and recommendations.
4. **Given** a scheduled reporting cadence, **When** the reporting period elapses, **Then** the system can produce the Executive Procurement Dashboard content in an exportable/reportable form for the reviewing executive.

### Edge Cases

- What happens when the Three-Way Match finds a quantity or pricing variance that falls just outside configured tolerance but is explainable (e.g., a supplier-approved partial substitution) — does the system allow a human reviewer to approve the exception with justification, or does it hard-block payment indefinitely with no override path?
- How does the system handle a Supplier ESG Compliance score that the supplier disputes as inaccurate — is there a defined appeal/re-assessment path, or does the AI-generated ESG score stand unchallenged and silently affect the supplier's Risk Rating and sourcing eligibility? [NEEDS CLARIFICATION: source names "ESG Compliance" and "ESG Risk" as monitored/scored dimensions but does not describe a dispute or re-assessment process.]
- What happens when a user who created and submitted a Purchase Request also holds an approver role in the same Approval Matrix — does the workflow engine block that user from approving their own request, or can a single individual satisfy multiple approval-chain roles for the same transaction? [NEEDS CLARIFICATION: Chapter 22 specifies an Approval Matrix, Parallel/Sequential Approvals, and RBAC/ABAC but does not use the explicit "Segregation of Duties" terminology found in the overlapping Chapter 24 content — see Assumptions.]
- What happens when a Purchase Order's cumulative value (including approved change requests) would exceed the referenced Budget Reference after initial Budget Validation already passed — does the PO lifecycle re-trigger Budget Validation and route for additional approval, or does it proceed on the originally validated budget figure?
- How does the system handle a Future Autonomous Procurement Agent (per the roadmap vision) generating a vendor-negotiation or purchase-approval recommendation that a human reviewer explicitly overrides — is the override captured with the same audit rigor as an accepted recommendation, and does the AI's confidence score or rationale remain attached to the overridden record for later review?
- What happens when the same supplier is simultaneously active in two competing RFQ/RFP sourcing events for overlapping procurement categories — does the platform prevent a conflict-of-interest or information leakage between the two competitive processes?
- How does the system handle an Emergency Purchase Order that must bypass the standard multi-day approval SLA due to urgency — does it still enforce a minimum approval chain (e.g., a single expedited approver) rather than skipping approval entirely, and how is the emergency justification captured for audit?
- What happens when a vendor is suspended or deactivated (Vendor Lifecycle stages 9–10) while it has open, in-flight Purchase Orders or goods already in transit — does the system block new POs to that vendor while still allowing existing in-transit deliveries and their three-way match/payment cycle to complete?
- How does AI-driven Inventory Procurement Automation behave when its demand forecast and the manually configured Reorder Point/Safety Stock rules disagree (e.g., AI predicts a demand spike the configured rule does not account for) — does the automated reorder defer to the human-configured rule, or can the AI recommendation increase the order quantity, and if so, does that still require human approval before the resulting Purchase Order is issued?

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

#### Foundation, Scope & Enterprise Procurement Lifecycle

- **FR-001**: System MUST provide a centralized, AI-powered procurement ecosystem managing the complete lifecycle of enterprise purchasing, vendor collaboration, sourcing, approvals, purchase orders, inventory replenishment, contract compliance, supplier performance, and spend governance, serving as the organization's single source of truth for procurement activities.
- **FR-002**: System scope MUST include Procurement Management, Purchase Request Management, Vendor Management, Supplier Intelligence, Strategic Sourcing, RFQ/RFP/RFI Management, Purchase Order Management, Contract Procurement, Procurement Approvals, Procurement Workflow Automation, Spend Management, Budget Validation, Inventory Procurement, Procurement Analytics, Vendor Performance Analytics, AI Procurement Intelligence, and an Executive Procurement Dashboard.
- **FR-003**: System MUST NOT replace ERP Finance Systems, Manufacturing Execution Systems, Warehouse Management Systems, Tax Filing Platforms, External Logistics Platforms, or Banking Platforms in its initial release; these systems MUST integrate securely through APIs, webhooks, message queues, and enterprise integration services.
- **FR-004**: System MUST progress every procurement activity through the standardized 17-step Enterprise Procurement Lifecycle (Purchase Requirement Identified → Budget Validation → Purchase Request Created → Approval Workflow → Vendor Identification → RFQ/RFP Issued → Vendor Evaluation → Vendor Selection → Purchase Order Creation → Order Confirmation → Goods Delivery → Goods Inspection → Invoice Matching → Payment Authorization → Vendor Performance Review → Procurement Analytics → Continuous Improvement), with each stage supporting configurable workflows, SLA monitoring, notifications, AI recommendations, approval history, and immutable audit logs.
- **FR-005**: System MUST keep every procurement activity fully visible, traceable, and auditable throughout its lifecycle (Transparency principle), and every purchase request MUST follow configurable procurement policies, approval workflows, financial limits, and compliance requirements (Policy-Driven Purchasing principle).

#### Enterprise Vendor Management

- **FR-006**: System MUST provide a centralized, AI-powered Vendor Management platform serving as the single source of truth for all supplier relationships, supporting at least 17 vendor categories (Product Suppliers, Service Providers, Software Vendors, Cloud Providers, Marketing Agencies, Training Partners, Consulting Firms, Logistics Providers, Manufacturers, Contractors, Freelancers, Financial Institutions, Legal Partners, Technology Partners, Channel Partners, Outsourcing Partners, Strategic Alliance Partners).
- **FR-007**: System MUST maintain a centralized Vendor Master Record for every vendor, including Vendor ID, Legal Business Name, Trade Name, Registration Number, Tax Identification, GST Details, PAN Information, Business Type, Industry Classification, Address details, Primary/Secondary Contacts, Banking Information, Payment Terms, Currency, Procurement Category, Risk Rating, Compliance Status, AI Trust Score, Vendor Status, and Audit History.
- **FR-008**: System MUST progress every vendor through the 11-stage Vendor Lifecycle (Registration → Initial Review → Document Verification → Qualification → Approval → Active → Performance Monitoring → Reassessment → Suspension → Deactivation → Archival), with each stage supporting configurable approvals, notifications, SLA monitoring, and complete audit history.
- **FR-009**: System MUST support Vendor Relationship Management covering vendor meetings, communication history, contract tracking, performance reviews, procurement history, purchase volume, risk history, escalation tracking, improvement plans, and strategic partnership management.
- **FR-010**: System MUST provide AI Vendor Intelligence generating Preferred Vendor Recommendations, Risk Alerts, Performance Trends, Procurement Optimization, Vendor Diversification Suggestions, Contract Renewal Recommendations, Supplier Consolidation Opportunities, Cost Optimization Insights, Vendor Health Scores, and Executive Procurement Insights, all as advisory output.

#### Supplier Information Management (SIM)

- **FR-011**: System MUST provide a Supplier Information Management (SIM) platform centralizing supplier data, compliance records, financial documents, certifications, operational capabilities, contracts, communication history, and business intelligence, ensuring consistent supplier information across every procurement process.
- **FR-012**: System MUST maintain, per supplier, a Supplier Information Repository (Company Overview, Corporate Structure, Registration/Tax/Compliance/Quality/Sustainability Certifications, Insurance Documents, Banking Information, Financial Statements, Service Portfolio, Product Catalog, Geographic Coverage, Manufacturing Capacity, Delivery Capabilities, Reference Customers, Audit Reports) and Supplier Documentation (Contracts, NDAs, Purchase Agreements, Quality Certifications, Compliance Reports, Audit Findings, Financial Statements, ESG Reports, Insurance Policies, Security Certifications).
- **FR-013**: System MUST enforce Data Governance over supplier information including version control, approval workflow, mandatory validation, expiration tracking, document renewal alerts, audit trail, data ownership, access control, encryption, and compliance monitoring; and MUST support Supplier Collaboration (secure messaging, document exchange, procurement notifications, PO updates, delivery status, invoice submission, issue resolution, meeting scheduling, performance feedback, supplier announcements).
- **FR-014**: System MUST provide AI SIM Intelligence generating missing document detection, compliance gap analysis, profile completeness score, supplier classification, duplicate detection, document expiry prediction, data quality recommendations, supplier benchmarking, risk notifications, and executive supplier insights, all as advisory output.

#### Supplier Qualification & Onboarding

- **FR-015**: System MUST automate supplier evaluation, risk assessment, compliance verification, onboarding approvals, and long-term supplier governance through a Supplier Qualification & Onboarding platform.
- **FR-016**: System MUST route every supplier through the standardized 10-step Qualification Workflow (Registration → Business Verification → Compliance Review → Financial Assessment → Technical Evaluation → Quality Assessment → Security Assessment → Risk Evaluation → Executive Approval → Onboarding), and MUST evaluate suppliers against the defined Qualification Criteria (Financial Stability, Business Reputation, Industry Experience, Product Quality, Delivery Capability, Compliance Status, Information Security, ESG Compliance, Innovation Capability, Scalability).
- **FR-017**: System MUST support Onboarding Automation (automated invitations, digital registration, e-signatures, document collection, identity verification, compliance validation, training assignment, portal provisioning, initial purchase approval, welcome notifications), and MUST continuously evaluate Supplier Risk across at least 10 categories (Financial, Operational, Compliance, Security, Delivery, Geographic, Legal, Political, ESG, Cybersecurity Risk).
- **FR-018**: System MUST provide AI Qualification Intelligence recommending Supplier Approval Scores, Qualification Readiness, Missing Requirements, Risk Mitigation Plans, Preferred Suppliers, Diversification Opportunities, Supplier Comparison, Procurement Strategy, and Vendor Ranking as advisory output; the Executive Approval step MUST remain a human decision that the AI recommendation informs but does not replace.

#### Strategic Sourcing Management

- **FR-019**: System MUST support Strategic Sourcing activities (Category Planning, Supplier Discovery, Supplier Shortlisting, Market Research, Spend Analysis, Cost Benchmarking, Competitive Bidding, Contract Negotiation, Award Recommendations, Supplier Allocation) across configurable procurement categories (at minimum Technology, Marketing, Infrastructure, Software, Cloud Services, Office Supplies, Training, Professional Services, Logistics, Manufacturing, Human Resources, Facilities).
- **FR-020**: System MUST support configurable Sourcing Strategies including Single Source, Dual Source, Multi Source, Global Sourcing, Local Sourcing, Sustainable Sourcing, Emergency Procurement, Strategic Procurement, Cost Optimization, and Innovation Partnerships.
- **FR-021**: System MUST provide AI Strategic Sourcing intelligence delivering Supplier Recommendations, Procurement Cost Forecasts, Category Optimization, Market Intelligence, Negotiation Insights, Procurement Opportunities, Demand Predictions, Supplier Capacity Forecasts, Sourcing Risk Analysis, and Executive Sourcing Insights, all as advisory output.

#### RFQ, RFP & RFI Management

- **FR-022**: System MUST provide RFQ (Request for Quotation), RFP (Request for Proposal), and RFI (Request for Information) management standardizing procurement requests while ensuring transparency, fairness, auditability, and intelligent supplier selection.
- **FR-023**: System MUST support RFQ Management (RFQ creation, supplier invitations, pricing templates, submission deadlines, bid comparison, clarification requests, evaluation workflow, vendor selection, approval workflow, award notifications) and RFP Management (scope definition, technical/business requirements, evaluation criteria, proposal submission, presentation scheduling, technical scoring, commercial scoring, final recommendation, executive approval).
- **FR-024**: System MUST support RFI Management (market research, supplier discovery, capability assessment, product information collection, technology evaluation, innovation identification, vendor prequalification, knowledge repository, supplier comparison, procurement planning) and MUST evaluate suppliers against the 10-criteria Evaluation Framework (Price, Quality, Technical Capability, Innovation, Delivery Performance, Financial Stability, Compliance, Sustainability, Risk Profile, Strategic Alignment).
- **FR-025**: System MUST provide AI Procurement Evaluation intelligence (automated proposal comparison, vendor ranking, commercial analysis, technical gap analysis, bid scoring, procurement risk detection, recommendation engine, procurement forecasting, decision support, executive evaluation reports) as advisory output; the final vendor award decision MUST be recorded as an explicit human/executive action.

#### AI Supplier Intelligence (Including ESG)

- **FR-026**: System MUST continuously analyze supplier performance, procurement history, commercial risk, financial health, compliance posture, sourcing efficiency, and strategic business value through an AI Supplier Intelligence platform, supporting Supplier Classification, Risk Prediction, Performance Benchmarking, Cost Optimization, Delivery Forecasting, Financial Health Analysis, Contract Intelligence, Procurement Forecasting, Supplier Diversification, and Strategic Procurement Planning.
- **FR-027**: System MUST continuously monitor Supplier Performance Intelligence across at least 10 dimensions — On-Time Delivery, Quality Performance, Procurement Cost, Invoice Accuracy, Contract Compliance, Issue Resolution Time, Innovation Contribution, Customer Satisfaction, **ESG Compliance**, and Procurement Efficiency — with ESG Compliance treated as a first-class, continuously scored input alongside operational and financial metrics, feeding into Supplier Risk Rating and Qualification Criteria (per FR-016).
- **FR-028**: System MUST deliver Executive AI Insights (Preferred Supplier Rankings, Procurement Health Index, Supplier Risk Dashboard, Spend Distribution, Strategic Sourcing Opportunities, Procurement Savings, Supplier Performance Trends, Procurement Compliance, AI Procurement Forecasts, Strategic Procurement Recommendations) to executives.
- **FR-029**: System MUST ensure all AI-generated procurement and supplier-intelligence recommendations remain explainable, configurable, continuously monitored, fully auditable, and subject to enterprise governance policies, consistent with constitution Article II.

#### Purchase Order Management

- **FR-030**: System MUST provide a centralized, policy-driven, AI-assisted Purchase Order (PO) platform for creating, approving, issuing, tracking, modifying, and closing purchase orders, supporting at least 12 PO types (Standard, Blanket, Contract, Planned, Service, Subscription, Emergency, Capital Procurement, Project Procurement, Internal Procurement, International, and Multi-Vendor Purchase Orders).
- **FR-031**: System MUST progress every Purchase Order through the 12-stage PO Lifecycle (Draft → Budget Validation → Approval → Purchase Order Generation → Supplier Acceptance → Fulfillment → Partial Delivery → Complete Delivery → Invoice Matching → Payment Authorization → Closure → Archival), with each stage supporting configurable workflows, SLA monitoring, notifications, escalation rules, AI recommendations, and immutable audit history.
- **FR-032**: System MUST maintain, for every Purchase Order, a complete record including Purchase Order Number, Request Number, Supplier Information, Delivery Address, Billing Information, Product/Service Details, Quantity, Unit Price, Taxes, Discounts, Shipping Charges, Delivery Schedule, Payment Terms, Currency, Budget Reference, Contract Reference, Approval History, Status, and Audit Trail.
- **FR-033**: System MUST provide AI Purchase Order Intelligence (PO prioritization, cost optimization, supplier selection support, delivery risk prediction, budget variance detection, duplicate order detection, approval recommendations, spend optimization, contract compliance monitoring, executive procurement insights) as advisory output.

#### Inventory Procurement Automation

- **FR-034**: System MUST automatically manage inventory replenishment, stock optimization, procurement planning, demand forecasting, and replenishment execution based on configurable business rules and predictive analytics.
- **FR-035**: System MUST continuously monitor Available Inventory, Reserved Inventory, Safety Stock, Reorder Point, Minimum/Maximum Stock, In-Transit Inventory, Backorders, Supplier Lead Time, and Warehouse Capacity, and MUST support configurable Automated Procurement Rules (automatic reorder, vendor selection, purchase request generation, purchase order creation, multi-warehouse procurement, seasonal inventory planning, emergency procurement, forecast-based procurement, low-stock alerts, overstock prevention).

### Key Entities *(include if feature involves data)*

- **Supplier / Vendor**: A registered business entity (product supplier, service provider, contractor, consultant, technology/logistics/channel partner, etc.) progressing through the 11-stage Vendor Lifecycle; identified by a unique Vendor Master Record.
- **Vendor Master Record**: The centralized profile for a vendor — legal/trade identity, tax/registration IDs, contacts, banking information, payment terms, procurement category, Risk Rating, Compliance Status, AI Trust Score, Vendor Status, and Audit History.
- **Supplier Profile (SIM Record)**: The Supplier Information Management repository for a supplier — company overview, corporate structure, certifications (registration, tax, compliance, quality, sustainability, security), financial statements, ESG reports, insurance documents, service/product portfolio, and audit reports, governed by version control, expiration tracking, and access control.
- **Supplier Qualification Record**: A supplier's progress through the 10-step Qualification Workflow, carrying evaluation results against the 10 Qualification Criteria (including ESG Compliance), AI Qualification Intelligence scores/recommendations, and the recorded Executive Approval decision gating Onboarding.
- **RFx (RFQ / RFP / RFI)**: A sourcing request instance — RFI (market research/discovery), RFQ (price-driven, with pricing templates and bid comparison), or RFP (scope/technical/commercial proposal evaluation) — carrying invited suppliers, submission deadlines, evaluation scores against the 10-criteria Evaluation Framework, AI-assisted comparison output, and the recorded award decision.
- **Purchase Request**: A requester-originated request for goods/services that must pass Budget Validation and the configured Approval Matrix before it can generate a Purchase Order.
- **Purchase Order (PO)**: A policy-driven procurement transaction of one of 12 PO types, progressing through the 12-stage PO Lifecycle, carrying supplier/delivery/billing details, pricing, budget/contract references, full Approval History, and immutable audit trail.
- **Goods Receipt**: A record of goods/services received against a Purchase Order (full or partial), including quality inspection results, warehouse allocation, and any damaged-goods or return-to-supplier disposition.
- **Three-Way Match Record**: The system-generated comparison of a Purchase Order, its Goods Receipt(s), and the Supplier Invoice across product, quantity, price, tax, delivery, contract, approval, and currency dimensions within configured tolerance; gates Payment Authorization and surfaces exceptions when out of tolerance.
- **Supplier ESG Score / Supplier Performance Score**: An AI-computed, continuously updated score across the 10 monitored performance dimensions (including ESG Compliance), feeding into Supplier Risk Rating, Qualification Criteria, and Preferred Supplier Rankings — advisory in nature, not a directly user-editable field.
- **Approval Workflow Step / Approval Matrix Entry**: A configured node in a multi-level (parallel and/or sequential) approval chain governing Purchase Requests, Purchase Orders, RFx awards, contracts, and payment authorizations; carries the assigned approver role, decision, timestamp, and rationale as part of the immutable audit trail.
- **Procurement Contract**: A Master Service Agreement, Framework Agreement, Purchase Contract, or other contract type progressing through the 11-stage Contract Lifecycle, carrying obligations, spend limits, renewal terms, and AI Contract Intelligence output.
- **AI Procurement Recommendation**: Any AI-generated output (vendor recommendation, sourcing insight, PO recommendation, contract insight, spend/inventory forecast, roadmap-stage autonomous-agent suggestion) carrying an explanation/confidence indicator and an explicit human-approval state — never self-executing for strategic, financial, or regulatory decisions.
- **Audit Record**: An immutable, timestamped, attributable entry logging vendor registration, purchase requests, purchase orders, contract changes, invoice processing, approval activities, payment requests, user actions, AI recommendations, security events, compliance reviews, and administrative activities.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: 100% of Purchase Requests pass through Budget Validation before entering the Approval Workflow, and 100% of resulting Purchase Orders retain a complete, immutable Approval History.
- **SC-002**: 100% of supplier payments are authorized only after a completed Three-Way Match (Purchase Order, Goods Receipt, Supplier Invoice) within configured tolerance; 0% of out-of-tolerance exceptions (quantity/price variance, duplicate invoice, missing delivery) result in automatic payment authorization.
- **SC-003**: 100% of suppliers reaching Active status have completed all 10 steps of the Qualification Workflow, including a recorded Executive Approval decision, with a unique Vendor Master Record maintained per supplier.
- **SC-004**: 100% of AI-generated procurement recommendations (vendor, sourcing, RFx evaluation, PO, contract, spend, inventory, and any roadmap-stage autonomous-agent output) carry an explanation/confidence indicator and remain advisory; 0% take a strategic, financial, or regulatory action (vendor award, budget commitment, contract signature, payment authorization) without a recorded human approval.
- **SC-005**: ESG Compliance is tracked as a continuously scored performance dimension for 100% of Active suppliers and demonstrably factors into Supplier Risk Rating and Preferred Supplier Rankings.
- **SC-006**: Procurement teams can execute a full competitive sourcing cycle (RFI/RFQ/RFP creation → supplier invitation → bid/proposal collection → AI-assisted evaluation → human-approved award) with a complete, retrievable audit trail for every stage.
- **SC-007**: 100% of vendor registration, purchase order, contract change, invoice processing, approval activity, payment request, AI recommendation, security event, compliance review, and administrative activity produce an immutable audit record that is not retroactively alterable by later configuration changes (consistent with constitution Article IV).
- **SC-008**: Inventory Procurement Automation's automatically generated reorder Purchase Requests/Purchase Orders pass through the same Budget Validation and Approval Workflow as manually created ones in 100% of cases — no automated reorder bypasses financial governance.
- **SC-009**: The platform sustains millions of purchase requests, purchase orders, and supplier records across multi-language, multi-currency, multi-region, high-availability deployment without degrading Procurement Portal or Executive Dashboard responsiveness.

## Assumptions

- **Overlap with Feature 057 (Volume 14, Chapter 24 — "procurement-supplier-management")**: Per `specs/FEATURE-MANIFEST.md`, Feature 057 (Chapter 24) covers substantially overlapping territory (vendor/supplier management, procurement workflow, approvals) and is flagged as redundant with this feature. **This feature (055, Chapter 22) is treated as the primary, detailed procurement specification** — it is longer (4 parts vs. Chapter 24's presumed shorter treatment), contains the full data models (Vendor Master Record, PO Record, Three-Way Match fields), and is the source of the canonical entity/requirement definitions for procurement, vendor management, sourcing, purchase orders, goods receipt, and spend analytics. **Feature 057's spec MUST cross-reference this feature's entities and functional requirements (Vendor/Supplier, Purchase Order, Approval Workflow, Three-Way Match, etc.) rather than re-deriving or duplicating them**, consistent with the constitution's Development Workflow directive that Volume 14 Chapters 24–40 "MUST cross-reference the overlapping feature(s) rather than duplicating requirements wholesale." Where Chapter 24 introduces the explicit "Segregation of Duties (SoD)" terminology that Chapter 22 does not use verbatim, Feature 057 is assumed to be the canonical source for that specific terminology/mechanic, while this feature (055) defines the Approval Matrix/RBAC/ABAC machinery that SoD is enforced through (FR-044, FR-055).
- **Segregation of Duties terminology**: Chapter 22 itself never uses the phrase "Segregation of Duties (SoD)" — it specifies an Approval Matrix, Parallel/Sequential Approvals, and RBAC/ABAC (Sections 20 and 24). This spec's FR-044 derives an SoD-equivalent control (a Purchase Request cannot be both submitted and approved by the same unauthorized actor) from those mechanisms and from constitution Article VII, and explicitly flags the precise enforcement mechanics as [NEEDS CLARIFICATION] pending reconciliation with Feature 057's fuller SoD specification.
- **Constitution Article II supersedes literal roadmap wording**: Section 29's statement that "human approval shall remain configurable for strategic, financial, and regulatory decisions" is treated in this spec as a mandatory, non-bypassable control rather than a toggle an administrator could disable, because constitution Article II states such approval gates are required, not optional. This is a deliberate interpretive choice, not a silent contradiction — see FR-065.
- Out-of-scope integrations (ERP Finance, Manufacturing Execution Systems, Warehouse Management Systems, Tax Filing Platforms, External Logistics Platforms, Banking Platforms) are assumed to be satisfied by existing/future platform features rather than built here; in particular, physical warehouse operations referenced by Inventory Procurement Automation (Section 18) are assumed to be the responsibility of Feature 056 (Enterprise Inventory & Warehouse Management/WMS), with this feature owning only the procurement-triggering (reorder rule → Purchase Request/Order) side of that boundary.
- Vendor payment execution and the underlying financial ledger are assumed to be handled by Feature 009 (Membership, Subscriptions, Payments & Revenue Operations), consistent with constitution Article V (Ledger-Based Internal Economies) — this feature authorizes payment requests but does not itself operate as the financial ledger/banking system.
- AI Trust Score, Supplier ESG Score, and other AI-computed scores are assumed to be system-computed/advisory outputs (not directly user-editable raw fields), consistent with the platform-wide AI governance principle; the source does not specify a numeric scoring formula for any of these scores.
- **[NEEDS CLARIFICATION]**: The source repeatedly requires "Executive Approval" and an "Approval Matrix" but does not specify approval thresholds (e.g., dollar amounts, discount percentages, or which of the 17 named Primary Users must approve which action/PO type). Per constitution Article VII, these thresholds and role-to-action mappings must be defined during planning.
- **[NEEDS CLARIFICATION]**: Three-Way Match "Tolerance Validation" is named as a control but no numeric tolerance values (e.g., acceptable % quantity/price variance) are specified in the source; these must be defined during planning.
- **[NEEDS CLARIFICATION]**: Budget overrun handling (e.g., automatic rejection vs. escalation vs. re-validation) is not detailed beyond the AI "Budget Variance Detection" capability; the exact workflow behavior when a PO's value would exceed its Budget Reference after initial validation must be defined during planning.
- **[NEEDS CLARIFICATION]**: A Supplier ESG Compliance dispute/appeal process is not described in the source; this must be defined during planning if ESG scores are to materially affect supplier risk rating and sourcing eligibility, as required by FR-027.
- Consistent with constitution Article II, all AI Supplier/Vendor/Sourcing/PO/Contract/Spend Intelligence and any future Autonomous Procurement Agent outputs in this feature are assumed to run server-side only, with no provider API key or system prompt ever exposed client-side, even though Chapter 22 does not restate this platform-wide rule explicitly.
- Portal authentication, RBAC foundation, and mobile experience for the Enterprise Procurement Portal (FR-052–FR-054) are assumed to reuse the platform's existing identity and design-system foundations established in Feature 003 (Auth, Identity, Onboarding) rather than defining a new identity system.
