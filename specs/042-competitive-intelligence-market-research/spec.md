## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - An Analyst Classifies and Prioritizes a New Competitor (Priority: P1)

A research analyst discovers a new organization competing for TBT's customers and needs to record it as a Competitor with the correct classification — Direct, Indirect, Replacement, or Aspirational — and let the system compute a configurable Priority Score from market overlap, segment overlap, product similarity, pricing similarity, geography, brand strength, growth rate, customer adoption, strategic threat, and innovation capability, so leadership can immediately see which competitors deserve the most monitoring attention.

**Why this priority**: The Competitor Profile Repository is the "central source of truth for all approved competitor intelligence" (§10–11) and every other competitive-intelligence capability in this chapter — product tracking, pricing, feature comparison, change monitoring, alerts — attaches to a classified competitor record. Without this, there is no foundation for the rest of the platform.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by creating a competitor profile, assigning it a classification (Direct/Indirect/Replacement/Aspirational), populating the priority-score inputs, and confirming the system computes a Priority Score and assigns a priority tier (Critical/High/Medium/Low/Watchlist Only) — independent of any product, pricing, or research data.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an analyst has identified a new organization, **When** they create a competitor profile with Competitor Name, Website, Headquarters, Business Category, Target Market, and the remaining §11 profile fields, **Then** the system saves the profile with status Draft and assigns a Research Owner.
2. **Given** a competitor profile exists, **When** the analyst classifies it as Direct, Indirect, Replacement, or Aspirational per the source definitions, **Then** the system applies the correct default monitoring priority (Direct competitors receive higher monitoring priority than other classes) and displays the classification on the profile.
3. **Given** a competitor's priority-score inputs (Market Overlap, Customer Segment Overlap, Product Similarity, Pricing Similarity, Geographic Relevance, Brand Strength, Growth Rate, Customer Adoption, Strategic Threat, Innovation Capability) are populated, **When** the score is calculated, **Then** the system classifies the competitor into Critical Priority, High Priority, Medium Priority, Low Priority, or Watchlist Only.
4. **Given** a competitor profile is marked High Priority, **When** a user without the required authorization attempts to activate, archive, or modify it, **Then** the system blocks the action, since only authorized users may activate, archive, or modify High Priority competitor records (§11).

### User Story 2 - An Analyst Tracks a Competitor Product Through Its Full Lifecycle (Priority: P2)

A product manager needs to track a specific competitor offering — a course, membership, or community product — from the moment it is first rumored through Announced, Beta, Publicly Available, Mature, Limited Availability, and eventually Discontinued or Replaced, with every stage change and every pricing/feature/partnership event landing on that product's timeline, and with significant changes triggering a Competitive Alert to relevant business teams.

**Why this priority**: Product Lifecycle Tracking and the Competitor Product Timeline (§13) are explicitly named enterprise capabilities that feed Change Monitoring (§19) and Competitive Alerts (§20). It is P2 because it depends on User Story 1's competitor record already existing, but it is the mechanism that keeps competitive intelligence current rather than a one-time snapshot.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by creating a competitor product record, advancing it through the eight lifecycle stages, recording timeline events (launches, feature releases, pricing changes, discontinuation), and confirming each transition appears in the Change Timeline with an assigned Impact Level and, where significant, generates a Competitive Alert routed through the alert workflow.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a competitor is being monitored, **When** an analyst records a new product with Product Name, Product Category, Delivery Model, Pricing Model, Main Features, and the remaining §13 fields, **Then** the system stores the product with lifecycle stage "Rumored" and Source References.
2. **Given** a competitor product's status changes from Beta to Publicly Available, **When** the analyst records the transition, **Then** the system logs the lifecycle change in the Competitor Product Timeline and in the Change Timeline with a Change Category, Description, and Business Impact.
3. **Given** a detected competitor change (e.g., a major feature release or pricing change) is classified with a Critical or High Impact Level, **When** the Competitive Alerts Platform processes it, **Then** the system runs the workflow Change Detected → Source Verified → Business Impact Calculated → Priority Assigned → Analyst Review (Optional) → Alert Published → Stakeholders Notified → Strategic Follow-up Created → Outcome Recorded, and delivers the alert through at least one configured channel (Admin Dashboard, Email, Push, Slack/Teams, Digest, or Report).
4. **Given** a competitor product reaches Discontinued or Replaced status, **When** the transition is recorded, **Then** the product remains visible in the historical timeline (it is not deleted) and the Customer Reaction field, if known, is captured.

### User Story 3 - Product and Marketing Compare TBT to Competitors Using the Feature Comparison Matrix (Priority: P1)

A product manager wants a single, continuously updated Feature Comparison Matrix — rather than manually screenshotting competitor websites — that shows, feature by feature, whether TBT and each named competitor (A–D) has it, how mature it is, and a competitive rating, and wants the system to automatically compute a Gap Analysis identifying features unique to TBT, features TBT is missing, features every competitor has, and high-priority development opportunities.

**Why this priority**: The Feature Comparison Matrix and automated Gap Analysis (§14–15) are named as directly supporting product roadmap planning, marketing positioning, and sales enablement — three of the platform's stated Business Objectives (§4, §10). It is a P1 because it is one of the platform's core deliverables independent of the research or persona subsystems.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by populating feature comparison rows for TBT and at least one competitor with Feature Status values, and confirming the Gap Analysis Dashboard automatically surfaces (a) features only in TBT, (b) features missing in TBT, (c) features in all competitors but not TBT, and (d) prioritized recommendations — without requiring any research or persona data.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a feature exists in the Feature Repository with Feature Name, Module, Category, and Feature Status, **When** it is added to the Feature Comparison Matrix, **Then** the row displays TBT Availability alongside up to four competitor columns (Competitor A–D), Feature Maturity, Competitive Rating, and Analyst Recommendation.
2. **Given** a feature's status is recorded as Fully Available for TBT and Not Available for all tracked competitors, **When** the Gap Analysis runs, **Then** the feature is surfaced as a unique competitive advantage.
3. **Given** a feature is Fully Available for three competitors and Not Available for TBT, **When** the Gap Analysis runs, **Then** the feature is surfaced as a missing/high-priority development opportunity, ranked using configurable business rules.
4. **Given** a feature comparison row has Business Value, Customer Demand, Competitive Importance, Development Complexity, Strategic Priority, Estimated ROI, and Innovation Score ratings recorded, **When** the Gap Analysis Dashboard is viewed, **Then** it uses those ratings, not just availability, to prioritize the recommendation.

### User Story 4 - Research Teams Conduct Primary and Secondary Research Under Research Governance (Priority: P1)

A research analyst needs to plan and run a customer interview, focus group, or survey (primary research) or organize an industry report (secondary research), and the platform must enforce Research Governance at every step — recording participant Consent Status before any participant data is used, routing the project through an approval workflow, versioning every document, and preventing permanent deletion of any approved research artifact — so no research activity happens outside enterprise compliance and ethical boundaries.

**Why this priority**: Research Governance (§28) is explicitly required to enforce "Participant Consent Management" and "Privacy Compliance," and the platform's Out-of-Scope section (§7) explicitly forbids collecting customer research data without required consent. This aligns directly with Constitution Article VI ("Consent Is First-Class, Per-Channel, and Versioned") and is a P1 because collecting research without governance is an ethical/legal violation, not merely a missing feature.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by creating a Primary Research project (e.g., a Customer Interview), attempting to record participant data without a recorded Consent Status (which must be blocked or flagged), completing the interview with consent recorded, and confirming the resulting research document is versioned, subject to approval, and cannot be permanently deleted once approved — independent of any competitor or persona data.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a research analyst creates a Primary Research project (Customer Interview, Survey, or Focus Group) with Project Name, Research Objective, Target Audience, Sample Size, and Research Method, **Then** the system requires the project to pass through the standardized lifecycle stages Research Request → Objective Definition → Research Design → Approval before Data Collection may begin.
2. **Given** a participant is being added to a research project, **When** their profile is created, **Then** the system requires a Consent Status field to be recorded, alongside Demographics, Customer Segment, Communication History, Participation History, Incentive Details, and Eligibility Status.
3. **Given** a completed customer interview generates a Transcript, Key Insights, Customer Quotes, and an AI Summary, **When** the AI Summary is produced, **Then** it is presented for review and editing by an authorized analyst before being treated as final (AI-generated summaries shall be editable by authorized analysts, §42).
4. **Given** a research document has been approved, **When** any user (including an administrator) attempts to permanently delete it, **Then** the system blocks the deletion — no approved research document shall be permanently deleted; archived versions remain available to authorized users for audit and historical reference (§28).

### User Story 5 - Product, Marketing, and Sales Work From AI-Assisted, Auto-Updating Customer Personas and Segments (Priority: P2)

A marketing manager wants research-backed Customer Personas (built from research findings, behavior, demographics, purchase history, and AI-assisted insights, not assumptions) and Market Segments that update automatically — without manual re-tagging — whenever a customer's purchase activity, product usage, engagement, membership status, or satisfaction changes significantly, so every team works from the same, current understanding of who TBT's customers are.

**Why this priority**: Persona Management and Market Segmentation (§29–30) are named platform-scope capabilities that explicitly forbid "relying on assumptions" and require AI-Based Dynamic Segmentation to auto-update segments on behavioral change. It is P2 because it depends on underlying customer/behavioral data already existing (from other platform features) and on the AI Assistant platform's model layer, but it directly feeds the Strategic Insights Dashboard and EDSS above it.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by creating a Customer Persona from research inputs, confirming an AI Confidence Score is attached, then simulating a significant change in a customer's Purchase Activity or Product Usage and confirming their assigned Market Segment updates automatically without a manual analyst action — independent of competitor or research-governance workflows.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** research findings and customer behavior data exist for a customer group, **When** an analyst builds a Customer Persona, **Then** the system requires the persona to include Demographics, Goals, Challenges, Motivations, Pain Points, Buying Behavior, and an AI Confidence Score, selecting from persona types such as Entrepreneur, Freelancer, Business Owner, Creator, or an administrator-defined custom type.
2. **Given** a customer is assigned to a Market Segment using Demographic, Behavioral, Firmographic, or another supported segmentation model, **When** a significant change occurs in that customer's Purchase Activity, Product Usage, Community Engagement, Learning Progress, Membership Status, Support Activity, Referral Behavior, or Customer Satisfaction, **Then** the AI segmentation engine automatically re-evaluates and updates their segment assignment.
3. **Given** a customer's segment assignment changes, **When** the Segment Comparison Dashboard is viewed, **Then** historical segment movement is retained for analysis rather than overwritten (§42).
4. **Given** the AI persona/segmentation service is unavailable, **When** a user views existing personas and segments, **Then** the system continues to display the last-computed persona and segment data (a deterministic fallback) rather than blocking access, consistent with Constitution Article II.

### User Story 6 - Leadership Reviews an Industry Benchmark Scorecard (Priority: P2)

An executive wants to see, at a glance, how TBT's Revenue Growth, Membership Growth, Retention, Churn, NPS, and other KPIs compare to industry averages and top performers, with every KPI classified as Industry Leader, Above Average, Industry Average, Below Average, or Critical Improvement Required, so leadership can immediately spot where TBT is falling behind and needs investment.

**Why this priority**: Industry Benchmarking (§31) is a named platform-scope capability with its own Acceptance Criteria requiring source references, update timestamps, and configurable KPIs. It is P2 because it is a comparison layer that depends on TBT's own performance data (owned by other features) plus externally sourced benchmark data, but it is a distinct, independently testable deliverable.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by loading a benchmark data set (TBT performance + industry average + top performer) for at least one KPI category and confirming the Benchmark Dashboard computes Gap Percentage and assigns the correct Benchmark Scorecard classification — independent of competitor, research, or persona data.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** benchmark data exists for a KPI category (e.g., Customer Retention) sourced from an Industry Report with a recorded source reference and last-verified date, **When** the Benchmark Dashboard is generated, **Then** it displays TBT Performance, Industry Average, Top Performer, Gap Percentage, Improvement Target, Trend Direction, and Benchmark Confidence.
2. **Given** TBT's Churn Rate is worse than the recorded Industry Average by a large margin, **When** the Benchmark Scorecard classifies that KPI, **Then** it is labeled "Critical Improvement Required" or "Below Average" rather than "Industry Average" or better.
3. **Given** a benchmark source's Expiration Review Date has passed, **When** an analyst or the system reviews the benchmark record, **Then** the record is flagged as needing re-verification rather than silently continuing to display as current.
4. **Given** an executive requests a Gap Analysis across multiple KPIs, **When** the dashboard is generated, **Then** it identifies improvement opportunities per KPI as required by the Benchmarking acceptance criteria (§42).

### User Story 7 - Leadership Evaluates Strategic Options Using Scenario Planning (Priority: P3)

A strategy team wants to model Best Case, Expected, Worst Case, Market Disruption, and other named scenarios — configuring inputs like Market Growth Rate, Pricing Changes, Marketing Budget, and Competitor Actions — and see estimated Revenue, Profitability, Customer Growth, Market Share, Risk, and Success Probability for each, with the AI comparing scenarios and proposing a preferred strategy that still requires executive approval before adoption.

**Why this priority**: Strategic Scenario Planning (§39) is an explicitly named capability that simulates outcomes "using research data and predictive analytics." It is P3 because it is the most forward-looking, judgment-dependent capability in the chapter, sitting on top of research, competitor intelligence, benchmarking, and predictive analytics that must already exist and be populated before scenario modeling produces meaningful output.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by configuring at least two scenarios (e.g., Expected vs. Worst Case) with different input assumptions, generating the estimated outputs for each, and confirming the AI-generated comparison/recommendation is clearly marked as requiring executive approval and does not itself alter any live budget, pricing, or roadmap record.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a strategist selects a scenario type (e.g., Market Disruption Scenario), **When** they configure Market Growth Rate, Customer Acquisition Rate, Pricing Changes, Marketing Budget, and Competitor Actions inputs, **Then** the system estimates Revenue, Profitability, Customer Growth, Market Share, Customer Retention, Business Risks, Resource Requirements, Strategic Opportunities, Expected ROI, and Success Probability for that scenario.
2. **Given** two or more scenarios have been configured for the same strategic question, **When** the AI engine compares them, **Then** it proposes a Preferred Strategy, Alternative Strategy, Risk Mitigation Plan, Investment Priorities, Execution Timeline, and Resource Allocation.
3. **Given** an AI scenario recommendation has been generated, **When** it is displayed to the strategy team, **Then** it is presented as requiring executive approval and takes no automatic action on any budget, pricing, or roadmap system.
4. **Given** a scenario's configured assumption (e.g., a projected Competitor Action) is later contradicted by an actual, observed market event, **When** an analyst reviews the scenario, **Then** the scenario can be flagged as having an invalidated assumption and re-run, rather than continuing to be presented as current guidance without qualification.

### User Story 8 - The Executive Decision Support System Never Executes Strategic Actions Automatically (Priority: P1)

An executive uses the Executive Decision Support System (EDSS) to review AI-generated strategic recommendations — launch a product, adjust pricing, enter a partnership, delay a high-risk investment — each backed by supporting evidence, source references, an AI confidence score, and a risk assessment, but every recommendation sits in a pending state until an authorized executive explicitly approves it; the system itself never executes the underlying business action.

**Why this priority**: This is the direct, explicit instantiation of Constitution Article II ("AI Is Assistive, Never Autonomous") in this chapter — the source states verbatim that "the system shall never execute strategic business actions automatically without explicit approval from authorized leadership" (§36). It is P1 because it is a governance/safety guarantee that every other AI-generated output in this chapter (pricing suggestions, opportunity recommendations, scenario recommendations, AI market intelligence) depends on, and any regression here is a constitutional violation, not a feature gap.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by generating an EDSS recommendation from existing research, competitor, benchmark, and performance data, confirming it displays Supporting Evidence, Source References, AI Explanation, Confidence Score, and Human Review Status, and confirming that no linked business system (pricing, roadmap, budget, partnership record) changes state until an authorized executive records an explicit approval action.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the EDSS has analyzed Customer Research, Market Research, Competitor Intelligence, Industry Benchmarks, Trend Analysis, Opportunity/Threat Assessments, Financial Performance, Product Performance, and Customer Experience Metrics, **When** it generates a recommendation (e.g., Adjust Pricing or Enter Strategic Partnership), **Then** the recommendation is displayed on the Executive Decision Dashboard with Strategic Objectives, Recommended Actions, Expected Business Impact, Investment Estimate, Risk Assessment, AI Confidence Score, and Decision Timeline, in a "pending approval" state.
2. **Given** an EDSS recommendation is pending, **When** no authorized executive has approved it, **Then** no linked business system (pricing engine, product roadmap, budget, partnership record) is modified as a result of the recommendation.
3. **Given** an authorized executive explicitly approves an EDSS recommendation, **When** the approval is recorded, **Then** the system logs the decision in the Decision History and Audit Trail together with an assigned Executive Owner and enables Progress Tracking and later Outcome Measurement.
4. **Given** an EDSS recommendation is rejected or left unapproved, **When** the recommendation's status is reviewed, **Then** it remains visible in the decision history as a non-executed recommendation rather than being silently discarded, preserving the full audit trail.

### Edge Cases

- What happens when an analyst or an automated collector attempts to gather competitor information via unauthorized system access, illegal private-data collection, bypassing website security controls, or purchasing confidential data from an unauthorized source? The platform's Out-of-Scope section (§7) explicitly forbids all four; the system MUST block or reject such collection attempts rather than merely discouraging them procedurally. [NEEDS CLARIFICATION: the source states these as scope exclusions/policy statements but does not define a technical enforcement or detection mechanism for blocking an analyst who manually enters data obtained this way.]
- How does the system handle a competitor Pricing record whose Last Verified Date has become stale (e.g., no re-verification for an extended period) — is it visually flagged as potentially outdated on the Pricing Comparison Dashboard, or does it continue to be treated as current? The source requires a Last Verified Date field (§16) and an Expiration Review Date for secondary-research sources (§23) but does not define a staleness threshold for pricing data specifically.
- What happens when a research participant withdraws consent after their interview or survey data has already been collected and incorporated into published research findings, personas, or an executive report? Research Governance (§28) requires Participant Consent Management, but the source does not specify whether withdrawal requires retroactively redacting the participant's contribution from already-published, approved research artifacts (which §28 also states may never be permanently deleted). [NEEDS CLARIFICATION: reconcile consent-withdrawal propagation (Constitution Article VI) with the "no approved research document shall be permanently deleted" rule.]
- How does the system respond when a Strategic Scenario Plan's configured assumption (e.g., "Competitor X will not expand into this region") is invalidated by a real, observed market event (e.g., a Competitive Alert reporting exactly that expansion)? The source does not define an automatic scenario-invalidation or re-run trigger tied to the Competitive Alerts Platform.
- What happens when the same competitor is independently created twice by two different analysts (duplicate competitor records), or when two analysts record conflicting pricing figures for the same competitor product from different sources on the same day? Layer 2 (§8) requires duplicate detection generally, but the source does not define the specific merge or conflict-resolution rule for competitor profiles or pricing records.
- How does the system handle an Indirect Competitor that is reclassified as a Direct Competitor mid-monitoring-cycle (§12) — does its monitoring priority, alert configuration, and historical change timeline carry forward unchanged, or does reclassification reset any tracked state?
- What happens when an AI-generated pricing recommendation, opportunity recommendation, or EDSS recommendation is generated but the responsible administrator/executive never acts on it (neither approves nor rejects) — does it expire, remain pending indefinitely, or escalate? The source requires approval before implementation/execution (§16, §33, §36) but does not define a timeout or escalation rule.
- How does the system handle a benchmark source (§31) that becomes unavailable or is later found to be unreliable after benchmark comparisons and Gap Analyses have already been generated and shared with executives — are dependent scorecards and reports automatically flagged for re-review?
- What happens when a Research Project's approval is rejected at the Approval lifecycle stage (§21) after Data Collection has already informally begun — is collected data discarded, quarantined pending a compliant re-approval, or retained under a documented exception?
- How does the system handle a Focus Group or Interview recording containing participant statements the AI discussion analysis (§25) flags as an emotional reaction or sensitive disclosure — is any additional confidentiality or redaction control applied beyond the standard Consent Status and access-permission model?

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Ethical Boundaries

- **FR-001**: System MUST NOT perform unauthorized access to competitor systems when collecting competitive intelligence (§7).
- **FR-002**: System MUST NOT collect private competitor information illegally (§7).
- **FR-003**: System MUST NOT bypass website security controls to collect competitor or market information (§7).
- **FR-004**: System MUST NOT purchase confidential data from unauthorized sources (§7).
- **FR-005**: System MUST NOT automatically publish strategic reports publicly; publication of any strategic report requires an explicit human action (§7).
- **FR-006**: System MUST NOT allow any AI-generated recommendation, forecast, or scenario output in this platform to make a final business decision without human approval (§7, §36; Constitution Article II).
- **FR-007**: System MUST NOT present this platform, its AI outputs, or its research findings as a replacement for legal, financial, or professional market advisors (§7).
- **FR-008**: System MUST NOT collect customer research data without required consent, and every competitive-intelligence and research activity MUST follow applicable laws, platform terms, privacy requirements, and TBT governance policies (§7; Constitution Article VI).

### Intelligence Architecture, Lifecycle & Core Principles

- **FR-009**: System MUST tie every strategic insight it surfaces to measurable data, research findings, customer evidence, or approved analyst observations, per the Evidence-Based Intelligence principle (§5.1).
- **FR-010**: System MUST continuously monitor configured competitors, market categories, customer signals, and industry developments rather than relying on one-time data pulls (§5.2).
- **FR-011**: System MUST maintain all approved market research and competitive intelligence in a centralized repository, and MUST display Information Source, Collection Date, Last Updated Date, Confidence Level, Analyst Notes, and Verification Status on every external insight (§5.3, §5.5).
- **FR-012**: System MUST restrict access to sensitive research, internal strategies, competitor assessments, and future business plans to authorized users only (§5.6; Constitution Article VII).
- **FR-013**: System MUST implement the six-layer intelligence architecture — Intelligence Sources, Data Collection & Validation, Intelligence Processing, Strategic Analysis, Business Action, Dashboards & Reporting — capturing source data, validating and routing uncertain/outdated information for verification, classifying and analyzing it, and surfacing approved outputs as roadmap recommendations, marketing tasks, pricing reviews, research projects, campaign opportunities, executive alerts, strategy-review tasks, or partnership investigations (§8).
- **FR-014**: System MUST process every intelligence record through the defined lifecycle — Information Identified, Source Recorded, Information Collected, Validation Performed, Classification Completed, Analyst Review Performed, Insight Generated, Business Impact Assessed, Recommendation Created, Decision Owner Assigned, Action Tracked, Outcome Measured, Intelligence Archived or Updated — so findings convert into measurable business action (§9).

### Competitor Classification & Tracking

- **FR-015**: System MUST maintain a Competitor Profile Repository as the central source of truth for approved competitor intelligence, with each profile capturing Competitor Name, Legal Business Name, Website, Logo, Headquarters, Operating Regions, Founded Year, Company Size, Business Category, Target Market, Target Customer Segments, Business Model, Revenue Model, Primary Products, Primary Services, Key Leadership Information, Public Contact Information, Social Media Profiles, Market Position, Estimated Market Strength, Threat Level, Research Owner, Last Reviewed Date, and Profile Status (§10–11).
- **FR-016**: System MUST support competitor profile statuses Draft, Under Research, Active, Monitoring, High Priority, Low Priority, Archived, and Verification Required, and MUST restrict activation, archiving, or modification of High Priority competitor records to authorized users only (§11).
- **FR-017**: System MUST support assigning administrator-defined tags (e.g., Community Platform, Learning Platform, Regional Competitor, Global Competitor, Emerging Competitor, High Threat, Strategic Reference) to competitors, with tag-based search, filtering, segmentation, and reporting (§11).
- **FR-018**: System MUST classify each competitor as Direct (similar products/services to similar customer segments), Indirect (solves the same customer problem via a different product or business model), Replacement (an alternative customers may use instead of purchasing a comparable platform), or Aspirational (an enterprise benchmark TBT does not directly compete with today), using the source's stated definitions and examples (§12).
- **FR-019**: System MUST assign Direct Competitors a higher default monitoring priority than other classifications, and MUST allow an Indirect Competitor to be reclassified as Direct as its business model evolves (§12).
- **FR-020**: System MUST track Replacement Competitor behaviors, since they may reduce customer willingness to purchase TBT memberships, and MUST support recording Aspirational Competitor study notes across UX, Community Design, Learning Systems, Personalization, Automation, Monetization, Customer Success, and Enterprise Scalability dimensions (§12).
- **FR-021**: System MUST calculate a configurable Competitor Priority Score from Market Overlap, Customer Segment Overlap, Product Similarity, Pricing Similarity, Geographic Relevance, Brand Strength, Growth Rate, Customer Adoption, Strategic Threat, and Innovation Capability, and MUST classify each competitor into Critical Priority, High Priority, Medium Priority, Low Priority, or Watchlist Only based on that score (§12). [NEEDS CLARIFICATION: the source lists the scoring inputs but does not define the weighting formula or numeric thresholds that separate the five priority tiers.]
- **FR-022**: System MUST support creating, updating, and archiving competitor records, assigning a competitor owner, adding research sources and analyst notes, generating competitor reports, configuring alerts, and maintaining a complete, versioned change history per competitor (§10, §42).

### Product Intelligence & Pricing History

- **FR-023**: System MUST maintain a Competitor Product record per offering capturing Product Name, Product Category, Product Description, Target Audience, Key Value Proposition, Delivery Model, Supported Platforms, Access Model, Pricing Model, Trial Availability, Main Features, Integrations, Support Options, Launch Date, Latest Update Date, Product Status, and Source References, classified into categories such as Free Community, Paid Community, Course, Membership, Coaching Program, Mobile Application, Webinar Platform, Event Platform, Creator Tool, CRM Tool, Marketing Tool, Business Education Platform, and Enterprise Subscription (§13).
- **FR-024**: System MUST track each competitor product through the lifecycle stages Rumored → Announced → Beta → Publicly Available → Mature → Limited Availability → Discontinued → Replaced, and MUST record every lifecycle stage change in the Competitor Product Timeline (§13).
- **FR-025**: System MUST maintain a Competitor Product Timeline recording Launches, Major Feature Releases, Pricing Changes, Rebranding, Product Expansion, Product Reduction, Partnerships, Discontinuation, and Customer Reaction, to help analysts understand the competitor's product strategy (§13).
- **FR-026**: System MUST maintain a competitor Pricing record per product/plan capturing Product Name, Plan Name, Currency, Monthly Price, Annual Price, Lifetime Price, Enterprise Pricing, Free Trial Availability, Free Plan Availability, Billing Cycle, Pricing Model (Free, Freemium, Monthly Subscription, Annual Subscription, One-Time Payment, Usage-Based, Tiered Pricing, Enterprise Quote, or Custom Pricing), Last Verified Date, and Source Reference (§16).
- **FR-027**: System MUST provide a Pricing Comparison Dashboard displaying Lowest Price, Highest Price, Average Market Price, Median Price, TBT Position, Competitor Distribution, Price Differences, and Value Comparison (§16).
- **FR-028**: The AI MAY suggest pricing actions (Reduce Pricing, Increase Pricing, Bundle Features, Launch New Plan, Add Free Trial, Improve Value Proposition, Introduce Enterprise Tier, Create Promotional Pricing), and every such recommendation MUST require administrator approval before implementation (§16; Constitution Article II).
- **FR-029**: System MUST maintain a Pricing History record for every detected pricing change capturing Previous Price, New Price, Percentage Change, Effective Date, Detected Date, Reason (if known), Analyst Notes, Source URL Reference, and Verification Status, and MUST generate pricing trend insights (Price Increase Frequency, Price Reduction Frequency, Seasonal Pricing, Promotional Pricing Patterns, Subscription Growth Trends, Annual Pricing Changes) plus a visual Pricing Timeline per competitor (§17).
- **FR-030**: System MUST track competitor promotional Offers (Percentage Discount, Fixed Discount, Coupon Code, Bundle Offer, Festival Campaign, Seasonal Promotion, Early Bird Offer, Referral Discount, Student Discount, Business Package, Free Trial Campaign, Limited-Time Offer) capturing Campaign Name, Start Date, End Date, Eligibility, Target Audience, Discount Type, Discount Value, Campaign Objective, Landing Page, Source Reference, and Current Status (§18).
- **FR-031**: System MUST allow analysts to record Offer Performance Notes (Customer Response, Social Engagement, Market Impact, Estimated Adoption, Competitive Risk, Lessons Learned) against tracked competitor offers (§18).

### Feature Comparison & Gap Analysis

- **FR-032**: System MUST maintain a Feature Repository where every record captures Feature Name, Module, Category, Description, Business Purpose, Availability, Feature Status, Launch Date, Last Updated, Feature Owner, Supporting Evidence, and Analyst Notes, grouped into categories including Community, Courses, Membership, Events, AI Features, Mobile App, Notifications, CRM, Marketing, Analytics, Automation, Payments, Integrations, Customer Support, Security, and Personalization (§14).
- **FR-033**: System MUST provide a Feature Comparison Matrix presenting each feature as a row with TBT Availability alongside up to four named competitor columns (Competitor A–D), Feature Maturity, Competitive Rating, and Analyst Recommendation, supporting Feature Status values Fully Available, Partially Available, Beta, Planned, Under Development, Not Available, and Unknown (§15).

### Key Entities *(include if feature involves data)*

- **Competitor**: A tracked organization with a Profile (§11), a Classification (Direct/Indirect/Replacement/Aspirational, §12), a computed Priority Score/Tier, administrator-defined Tags, an assigned Research Owner, and a versioned change history; the parent record for all Products, Pricing, Feature Comparisons, Change Timeline entries, and Alerts associated with that organization (§10–20).
- **Competitor Product**: A tracked offering (course, membership, community, tool, etc.) belonging to a Competitor, with Category, Delivery/Access/Pricing Model, Main Features, a Lifecycle Stage (Rumored→Replaced), and a Product Timeline of launches, feature releases, pricing changes, and discontinuation events (§13).
- **Pricing Record / Pricing History Entry**: A snapshot of a Competitor Product's price (plan, currency, billing cycle) with a Last Verified Date and Source Reference, plus historical entries capturing Previous/New Price, Percentage Change, Effective/Detected Date, and Verification Status, feeding the Pricing Comparison Dashboard and Pricing Timeline (§16–17).
- **Competitor Offer**: A tracked promotional campaign (discount, coupon, bundle, seasonal offer) run by a competitor, with Campaign Name, dates, eligibility, discount details, and analyst-recorded Offer Performance Notes (§18).
- **Feature Comparison Matrix Entry**: A row comparing one Feature across TBT and up to four competitors, with Feature Status, Feature Maturity, Competitive Rating, Analyst Recommendation, and the ratings (Business Value, Customer Demand, Competitive Importance, Development Complexity, Strategic Priority, Estimated ROI, Innovation Score) that drive the automated Gap Analysis (§14–15).
- **Competitor Change / Competitive Alert**: A detected competitor event (new feature, pricing change, partnership, funding, etc.) with a Change Category, Business Impact/Impact Level, and Supporting Evidence, that may generate an Alert routed through the Alert Workflow to configured stakeholders and channels (§19–20).
- **Research Project / Research Study**: A Primary or Secondary Research effort (interview, survey, focus group, or organized external source) that moves through the standardized Research Lifecycle (Request→Archive) under Research Governance, with a Research Owner, Timeline, Budget, and Expected Deliverables (§21–23).
- **Research Participant**: An individual enrolled in Primary Research with Demographics, Customer Segment, a mandatory Consent Status, Communication/Participation History, Incentive Details, and Eligibility Status (§22).
- **Customer Interview / Focus Group / Survey**: Structured research-method records (moderator, guide/questions, schedule, consent) that produce Transcripts, Key Insights, Discussion Summaries, or Survey Analytics, with AI-generated summaries subject to analyst review and edit (§24–26).
- **Research Repository Document**: An approved research artifact (project, interview note, survey result, focus group report, benchmark report, persona, segmentation study, executive presentation, or recommendation) stored with version control, tags, access permissions, and an audit log; never permanently deletable once approved (§27–28).
- **Customer Persona**: A research-backed profile of a customer archetype (e.g., Entrepreneur, Freelancer, Creator) with Demographics, Goals, Challenges, Motivations, Pain Points, Buying Behavior, and an AI Confidence Score, auto-summarizing common goals, frustrations, and decision factors (§29).
- **Market Segment**: A group of customers/prospects classified by a segmentation model (Demographic, Behavioral, Firmographic, AI-Based Dynamic, etc.) with Population Size, Revenue Contribution, Engagement/Retention/Satisfaction/Churn metrics, and Strategic Priority, that the AI engine may re-assign automatically on significant behavioral change (§30).
- **Benchmark Scorecard Entry**: A KPI's comparison against Industry Average and Top Performer, with Gap Percentage, Trend Direction, Benchmark Confidence, and a classification (Industry Leader through Critical Improvement Required) (§31).
- **Trend**: An identified emerging pattern with a Category, Source, Growth Rate, Confidence Level, and a lifecycle position (Emerging→Archived) (§32).
- **Opportunity**: An identified business opportunity with Estimated Market Size, Customer Demand, Investment Required, Revenue Potential, Strategic Fit, Risk Level, Priority, Owner, and Status (§33).
- **Threat**: An identified risk with Probability, Business Impact, Urgency, Risk Score, Recommended Mitigation, and a Responsible Owner, classified on the Risk Matrix (§34).
- **Executive Decision / EDSS Recommendation**: An AI-generated strategic recommendation (e.g., Adjust Pricing, Enter Strategic Partnership) carrying Supporting Evidence, AI Explanation, Confidence Score, Risk Assessment, Human Review Status, and Decision History; requires explicit executive approval before any linked business system changes (§36).
- **Scenario Plan**: A configured strategic scenario (Best Case, Worst Case, Market Disruption, etc.) with input assumptions (growth rate, pricing, budget, competitor actions) and estimated outputs (Revenue, Market Share, Risk, ROI, Success Probability), comparable against other scenarios via AI-generated recommendations requiring executive approval (§39).
- **Executive Report / Intelligence Briefing**: A scheduled or on-demand output (Daily Brief through Annual Outlook) assembled from Market Highlights, Competitor Updates, Customer Insights, Trends, Opportunities, Risks, and AI Recommendations, distributed through the Executive Dashboard or export channels (§40).

## Assumptions

- The ethical-boundary constraints in §7 (no unauthorized competitor-system access, no illegal private-data collection, no bypassing website security controls, no purchase of confidential data from unauthorized sources, no customer research without consent) are treated in this spec as hard, non-negotiable functional requirements (FR-001–FR-008) rather than advisory policy statements, consistent with the constitution's "No Dark Patterns" and consent principles. Any implementation plan that would require any of these behaviors must be rejected, not merely flagged.
- AI-driven Customer Persona generation (§29) is assumed to depend on the TBT AI Assistant platform (`008-ai-assistant-platform`) for its underlying model/inference layer; this spec defines the persona data model, governance, and human-review requirements but assumes the AI Assistant feature owns the shared AI infrastructure, prompt governance, and provider-key handling per Constitution Article II.
- AI-Based Dynamic Segmentation (§30) and the AI Market Intelligence Platform (§37) are assumed to consume customer/behavioral data already modeled by the Audience Segmentation & CDP feature (`019-audience-segmentation-cdp`) and Enterprise Segmentation & Audience Intelligence feature (`035-enterprise-segmentation-audience-intelligence`) rather than re-implementing a separate customer data store; this spec defines the competitive-intelligence-specific segment attributes and dynamic-update behavior on top of that shared data.
- Sales Data, CRM Data, and Membership/Course Performance data referenced as Intelligence Sources (§8 Layer 1) and AI Intelligence Sources (§37) are assumed to be sourced from the existing CRM (`013-crm-sales-support`) and Membership, Payments & Revenue (`009-membership-payments-revenue`) features rather than duplicated within this platform.
- Per repository convention (`CLAUDE.md`), Volume 14's later chapters are uneven in specification depth; this chapter is largely a flat, list-based enumeration of fields and capabilities without full field-level data types, numeric thresholds, or error codes. Where the source names a capability without fully defining its behavior (e.g., Competitor Priority Score weighting, consent-withdrawal-vs-immutability reconciliation, scenario-invalidation triggers, approval timeout/escalation), this spec extracts only what is stated and flags the gap with `[NEEDS CLARIFICATION: ...]` rather than inventing the missing rule.
- The next chapter (Volume 14 – Part 2 – Chapter 10, feature `043-product-strategy-innovation-management`) is assumed to own Product Roadmap execution; this spec's Opportunity Identification and Gap Analysis outputs are assumed to hand off *recommendations* into that feature's roadmap-planning workflow rather than this platform directly managing the product roadmap itself.
- Compliance frameworks invoked by Research Governance (§28) — Privacy Compliance, Regulatory Compliance — are assumed to sit alongside, not replace, the full compliance baseline in the Constitution's "Security & Compliance Baseline" (GDPR, CCPA, DPDP Act, ISO 27001, SOC 2, PCI DSS), which this chapter does not restate but which is assumed to still apply platform-wide.
- Data retention periods for competitor intelligence, research artifacts, and AI recommendation history are stated as "configurable" (§28, §42) without a specified default; actual retention durations are assumed to be an administrative/deployment-time configuration decision, not a spec-level requirement.
