## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Customer Progresses Through the 15-Stage CX Lifecycle (Priority: P1)

A prospective member is first captured as a Visitor, and as they engage with Tamil Business Tribe (browsing the site, registering, trialing, purchasing, onboarding) the platform automatically tracks and advances them through the standardized 15-stage Customer Experience lifecycle (Visitor → Lead → Prospect → Trial User → New Customer → Onboarding → Activated User → Engaged Customer → Loyal Customer → Advocate → Renewal → Expansion → Win-Back → Alumni → Archived), each stage carrying its own KPIs, engagement strategy, and automation rules that the CX and Customer Success teams can configure.

**Why this priority**: The 15-stage lifecycle is the foundational data model every other CXOS capability (journey mapping, health scoring, playbooks, dashboards) is built on top of. Without a reliable, current lifecycle-stage assignment per customer, no other CX capability in this chapter can function. This is the minimum viable slice of the CXOS.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by creating a test customer record, driving it through registration → trial → purchase → cancellation, and verifying at each step that the system assigns the correct lifecycle stage, exposes configurable KPIs/automation rules for that stage, and that the stage is visible on the Customer Health Dashboard — independent of journey mapping, VoC, or governance features being built.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an anonymous website visitor with no account, **When** they submit a lead-capture form, **Then** the platform creates a customer record at the "Lead" stage and exposes that stage's configured KPIs and automation rules.
2. **Given** a customer at the "Trial User" stage who completes a paid purchase, **When** the purchase is confirmed by the payment system, **Then** the platform transitions the customer to "New Customer" and then "Onboarding" per configured stage-entry criteria.
3. **Given** a customer at the "Engaged Customer" stage who stops all activity for a configured inactivity threshold, **When** the inactivity threshold is crossed, **Then** the platform flags the customer for Win-Back stage evaluation rather than silently leaving them at "Engaged."
4. **Given** an authorized CX administrator, **When** they open lifecycle-stage configuration, **Then** they can view and edit KPIs, engagement strategies, and success metrics for each of the 15 stages without a software change.

### User Story 2 - CX Team Maps and Analyzes a Customer Journey with AI-Detected Friction Points (Priority: P1)

A Customer Experience Director creates a journey map for the "Customer Onboarding Journey," defining its stages (Awareness through Advocacy), the customer goals/actions/emotions/pain points at each stage, and reviews the journey using any of 8 visualization modes (Timeline, Flow Diagram, Swimlane, Persona, Channel, Heat Map, Sankey Flow, Interactive Canvas). AI-driven Journey Analytics highlights bottlenecks, friction points, and drop-off predictions so the team can prioritize where to intervene.

**Why this priority**: Journey Mapping and Journey Analytics are the primary working tool for CX/product/marketing teams to diagnose and fix experience problems, and directly feed Journey Optimization and the Journey Intelligence Dashboard. It is P1 because it is the second most foundational capability after lifecycle tracking, and is independently valuable even before Customer Success or VoC capabilities exist.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by creating one journey map end-to-end (name, persona, stages, elements), publishing it through the journey approval workflow, viewing it in each of the 8 visualization modes, and confirming Journey Analytics surfaces at least one AI-identified friction point with supporting data — independent of the Customer Success or VoC platforms.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a CX Director drafting a new journey map, **When** they add stages and populate journey elements (goals, actions, emotions, pain points, opportunities) for each stage, **Then** the platform saves the map and makes it available in all 8 visualization modes.
2. **Given** a published journey with sufficient behavioral data, **When** the AI Journey Analytics engine runs, **Then** it surfaces journey bottlenecks, friction points, and drop-off predictions tied to specific stages.
3. **Given** a journey map that has been revised, **When** the revision is saved, **Then** the platform retains complete version history and an audit record of the change.
4. **Given** an AI recommendation to "remove friction" at a specific journey stage, **When** a CX team member reviews it, **Then** the recommendation requires explicit configurable review/approval before it can be implemented — it is never applied automatically.

### User Story 3 - Customer Success Manager Monitors Health Score and Receives an Auto-Triggered Playbook (Priority: P1)

A Customer Success Manager (CSM) monitors a portfolio of accounts through the Customer Health Score, which blends 14 weighted categories (Product Usage, Login Frequency, Feature Adoption, Learning Progress, Community Participation, Support History, Payment Status, Subscription Status, Customer Satisfaction, NPS, Renewal Probability, AI Engagement, Goal Achievement, Account Activity) into one of 6 health tiers (Excellent, Healthy, Stable, Attention Required, At Risk, Critical). When an account's score crosses into "At Risk," a configured Success Playbook (e.g., "High-Risk Customer Recovery") automatically triggers, assigning recommended actions to the CSM.

**Why this priority**: Health Scoring and Playbooks are the operational core of the Customer Success Platform — the single mechanism translating raw signal into proactive human action, directly tied to the constitution's "AI Is Assistive, Never Autonomous" principle (automation surfaces the playbook; a human executes it). It is P1 because churn prevention is a stated Mission-Critical business objective of this chapter.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by seeding a test account with declining usage and a missed payment, confirming the blended health score recalculates into a lower tier with the correct sub-score contributions visible, and confirming the configured playbook's trigger condition fires and creates a reviewable task for the assigned CSM — independent of journey mapping or VoC.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a customer account with configured category weights, **When** underlying usage, payment, NPS, and AI-engagement data changes, **Then** the platform recalculates the blended health score and reassigns the customer to the correct one of the 6 health tiers.
2. **Given** an account whose health score crosses a configurable "At Risk" threshold, **When** the threshold is crossed, **Then** the platform automatically evaluates configured playbook trigger conditions and, if matched, creates the playbook's recommended actions for the assigned team — subject to configurable automated-execution review.
3. **Given** an AI health prediction (e.g., "Renewal Risk"), **When** it is surfaced to a CSM, **Then** it includes a confidence score and supporting evidence.
4. **Given** an account newly flagged as high churn risk, **When** the flag is raised, **Then** the account automatically appears in the Customer Success review queue.

### User Story 4 - CSM Builds and Executes a Success Plan from a Template (Priority: P2)

A CSM assigns a "New Customer" Success Plan template to a newly onboarded enterprise account, customizes the goals/milestones/action items/KPIs/target dates, and the platform automatically schedules reviews, creates follow-up tasks, sends milestone reminders, and escalates overdue action items.

**Why this priority**: Success Plans operationalize the strategy behind a health score/playbook signal into a trackable, milestone-based customer-specific plan. It is P2 because it depends on the Customer Success Lifecycle and Health Scoring (P1) already existing, and because a health-score-only MVP can ship value before formal plan templating is required.

**Independent Test**: Can be tested by assigning a template to one test account, editing its milestones and target dates, letting a milestone go overdue, and confirming the platform auto-escalates it — independent of playbooks or VoC.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a new enterprise customer, **When** a CSM assigns the "Enterprise Customer" success plan template, **Then** the plan is created with configurable goals, milestones, action items, KPIs, dependencies, risks, and a review schedule.
2. **Given** an active success plan with a scheduled milestone review, **When** the review date arrives, **Then** the platform automatically creates the review task and notifies the assigned CSM.
3. **Given** a success plan action item past its target date, **When** the target date passes without completion, **Then** the platform automatically escalates the overdue action.

### User Story 5 - Customer Progresses Through the 17-Stage Customer Lifecycle with Dynamic Segmentation (Priority: P2)

A customer moves through the broader 17-stage Customer Lifecycle Management (CLM) model (Anonymous Visitor → Marketing Lead → Qualified Lead → Prospect → Registered User → Trial User → Paying Customer → Active Customer → Power User → Premium Member → Enterprise Customer → Advocate → Referral Partner → Dormant Customer → Churned Customer → Win-back Customer → Alumni), while the platform continuously recalculates their dynamic segment membership (behavioral, revenue, product-usage, lifecycle segments) and applies the matching persona for personalization.

**Why this priority**: CLM, Segmentation, and Persona are cross-functional (marketing, sales, product, CS) and drive personalization across the whole platform, but are P2 because they extend rather than replace the CX Lifecycle (User Story 1) and can be layered on afterward.

**Independent Test**: Can be tested by driving one test customer through several CLM stage transitions, confirming lifecycle automation (onboarding trigger, CS task assignment, engagement campaign launch) fires at each transition, and confirming their dynamic segment membership updates near-real-time as their purchase/usage data changes.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a Registered User who completes a purchase, **When** the purchase is confirmed, **Then** the platform transitions them to "Paying Customer" and triggers the configured onboarding workflow.
2. **Given** a customer whose product usage and Customer Health Score change, **When** the underlying data updates, **Then** the platform recalculates their dynamic segment membership (e.g., moving from "Growing" to "At-Risk" revenue segment) in near real time.
3. **Given** a customer matched to the "Entrepreneur" persona, **When** personalized content or recommendations are generated for them, **Then** the persona's goals, pain points, and channel preferences inform the personalization.

### User Story 6 - CX Team Identifies and Nurtures an Advocate via AI Signals and Loyalty Program (Priority: P2)

The AI engine identifies a highly satisfied, long-retained customer with high NPS, high product usage, and community leadership as a potential Advocate. The CX team invites them into the Advocacy Platform (testimonials, case studies, referral campaigns) while the Loyalty Management platform tracks their points, tier, and redeemable benefits.

**Why this priority**: Advocacy and Loyalty are downstream, high-value outcomes of a healthy customer relationship, and are appropriately P2/P3 — valuable but dependent on the lifecycle, health scoring, and engagement capabilities already existing.

**Independent Test**: Can be tested by seeding a test account with high NPS, high usage, and referral activity, confirming the AI advocate-identification signals surface the account for review, and confirming a manually approved referral produces a correctly tracked reward through the referral workflow (Invitation → Registration → Qualification → Conversion → Reward Approval → Reward Distribution → Performance Tracking).

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a customer with high NPS, high product usage, and consistent community leadership, **When** the AI advocate-identification job runs, **Then** the customer is surfaced as a potential advocate for CX team review.
2. **Given** an approved advocate, **When** they submit a referral, **Then** the referral progresses through all 7 defined workflow stages with fraud-detection checks before reward distribution.
3. **Given** a loyalty program member, **When** they redeem loyalty points for a configured benefit, **Then** the platform records the redemption against their loyalty metrics.

### User Story 7 - VoC Team Consolidates 20 Feedback Sources Through a 10-Step Workflow (Priority: P1)

The Voice of Customer team monitors feedback arriving from up to 20 configured data sources (mobile app, web portal, AI assistant, community, support, email, WhatsApp, push responses, in-app feedback, surveys, polls, course/webinar/event feedback, product/app-store reviews, social mentions, referral feedback, customer interviews, executive business reviews). Each feedback item is classified into a voice category, scored for sentiment, prioritized, and tracked through a 10-step workflow (Submitted → Acknowledged → Reviewed → Prioritized → Assigned → In Progress → Implemented → Verified → Closed → Archived) until resolution.

**Why this priority**: VoC is a Mission-Critical, chapter-wide "central source of customer intelligence" per the source PRD, feeding health scoring, journey optimization, and executive dashboards alike. It is P1 alongside lifecycle tracking and health scoring because customer-reported signal is foundational input to both.

**Independent Test**: Can be tested by submitting one feedback item through each of several channels (survey, support ticket, in-app), confirming each is classified into a voice category with a sentiment score, and driving one item through the full 10-step workflow to "Closed," confirming an immutable audit log exists for every transition.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** feedback submitted through a survey, support ticket, and in-app form, **When** each is received, **Then** the platform records a feedback item with category, sentiment score, priority, journey stage, and assigned team.
2. **Given** a feedback item at "Prioritized" status, **When** an authorized team member assigns it, **Then** it transitions to "Assigned" and the transition is captured in an immutable audit log.
3. **Given** open-text feedback from a support ticket, community post, and product review, **When** the AI sentiment engine processes them, **Then** each is classified into one of the 6 sentiment categories (or a custom label) with emotion detection, topic extraction, and confidence-scored escalation recommendations.
4. **Given** an NPS/CSAT/CES survey, **When** it is built using the survey designer, **Then** it supports drag-and-drop question building, conditional branching logic, and multi-language delivery, and can be distributed across all 9 configured channels.

### User Story 8 - CX Governance Committee Reviews an AI-Recommended Journey Change (Priority: P3)

The CX Governance Committee (Chief Customer Officer, CX Director, Product Manager, Compliance Officer, UX Lead, and others) reviews an AI-generated journey-optimization recommendation and an associated ethical-AI/accessibility concern before it is approved for publication, ensuring the change aligns with enterprise standards for quality, consistency, accessibility, privacy, and ethical AI use.

**Why this priority**: Governance is a cross-cutting control layer rather than a standalone value-delivering capability; it is essential for compliance and audit but is reasonably deferred (P3) until the journey, health-score, and VoC capabilities it governs already exist and are producing recommendations to review.

**Independent Test**: Can be tested by submitting one journey-optimization recommendation through the governance workflow (Journey Approval → Compliance Review → Accessibility Review → Executive Approval), confirming a governance role can approve, reject, or request changes, and confirming every governance action is captured in an immutable audit log and reflected in the Governance Dashboard's compliance score.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a pending journey-optimization recommendation, **When** it is submitted for governance review, **Then** it routes through Journey Approval, Compliance Review, and Accessibility Review before Executive Approval.
2. **Given** a governance reviewer with the Compliance Officer role, **When** they reject a recommendation on privacy grounds, **Then** the recommendation is blocked from publication and the rejection reason is logged immutably.
3. **Given** an AI Model Review activity scheduled on the governance calendar, **When** it is conducted, **Then** findings are recorded and reflected in the Governance Dashboard's audit-findings and compliance-score components.

### Edge Cases

- What happens when a customer's Health Score sub-scores conflict — e.g., Product Usage and Feature Adoption are "Excellent" while Payment Status is "Critical" (a failed renewal payment)? The spec does not state a documented tie-breaking or floor/cap rule for how a single critically failing category affects the blended tier; this MUST be clarified in configuration rather than left to an undocumented weighted average. [NEEDS CLARIFICATION: does any single critical sub-score (e.g., Payment Status, Subscription Status) force a floor/cap on the overall health tier regardless of weighted average, or is it purely additive?]
- What happens when two different Success Playbooks are triggered for the same account in the same window (e.g., "Low Health Score" and "Missed Milestones" both fire "High-Risk Customer Recovery" and "Activation Recovery" simultaneously)? The platform must not create duplicate, conflicting, or redundantly overlapping outreach/task assignments for the same account and same underlying issue.
- What happens when a Voice of Customer signal (e.g., a negative support-ticket sentiment score) directly contradicts a recent structured survey response (e.g., a high CSAT score) for the same customer in the same period? The platform must have a defined reconciliation or "conflicting signal" flagging behavior rather than silently averaging or silently favoring one source.
- What happens when the CX Governance Committee rejects or overrides a journey/experience change that AI already recommended and a downstream team already began implementing based on the (unapproved) recommendation? The governance rejection must be able to block/roll back the in-flight change, and the rejection must be traceable to the specific recommendation and reviewer.
- What happens when a single customer is simultaneously eligible for two different, potentially conflicting journeys (e.g., both a "Renewal Journey" and a "Win-back Journey" fire because their subscription is expiring while their usage has also gone dormant)? The platform must define precedence/conflict rules rather than enrolling the customer in contradictory journeys concurrently.
- What happens when a customer's Dynamic Segment membership changes mid-flight while an engagement campaign or automated journey targeting that segment is already in progress for them? The system must define whether in-flight automation continues to completion or halts/re-evaluates when segment membership changes in near real time.
- What happens when a single feedback item legitimately spans multiple Voice Categories (e.g., a complaint that is simultaneously "Pricing Feedback" and "Technical Issue" and "Feature Request")? The platform must support multi-category classification/routing rather than forcing a single category that causes the item to be under-routed.
- What happens when the AI engine's Journey Optimization recommendation for a customer contradicts the Customer Success Playbook's recommended next action for the same customer at the same time (e.g., journey AI recommends "simplify workflow / reduce touchpoints" while the playbook recommends "schedule an Executive Business Review")? Conflicting AI-generated next-best-actions for the same customer must be surfaced together for human reconciliation, not executed independently by two automations.
- What happens when a customer withdraws marketing/communication consent for a channel (per the platform-wide consent principle) while they are mid-lifecycle inside an active Success Plan, Playbook, or Journey automation that sends communications on that channel? The withdrawal must propagate immediately and halt in-flight sends on that channel without delay, even though the lifecycle/plan/playbook itself continues.
- What happens when a customer's Lifecycle Stage would advance to "Advocate" or "Renewal" (per automated stage-transition rules) while they simultaneously have an open, unresolved, high-priority Feedback item (e.g., an escalated complaint) still "In Progress"? The platform must decide whether unresolved high-severity feedback should block or flag an otherwise-qualifying lifecycle/advocacy transition rather than silently advancing a dissatisfied customer to "Advocate."

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

#### CX Lifecycle & Operating Model

- **FR-001**: System MUST continuously measure, analyze, optimize, and improve every stage of the customer experience rather than treating customer interactions as isolated events, providing a unified view of the complete customer relationship from first marketing touchpoint to long-term advocacy.
- **FR-002**: System MUST personalize customer experiences based on Customer Profile, Membership Level, Product Usage, Learning Progress, Purchase History, Community Activity, Customer Goals, and Behavioral Signals.
- **FR-003**: System MUST support continuous improvement of customer experiences using Customer Feedback, Behavioral Analytics, AI Insights, Product Analytics, and Customer Success Data.
- **FR-004**: System MUST deliver a consistent customer experience across Mobile Application, Web Portal, Community, Email, WhatsApp, Push Notifications, Customer Support, Live Events, Courses, and AI Assistants.
- **FR-005**: System MUST collect, process, and store customer information according to enterprise privacy and governance policies, and MUST keep final customer-impacting decisions configurable and reviewable by authorized users even where AI assists with personalization, journey analysis, segmentation, success predictions, churn prediction, recommendations, or automated engagement.
- **FR-006**: System MUST implement the defined Platform Scope: Customer Journey Mapping, Customer Lifecycle Management, Customer Success Management, Customer Health Scoring, Customer Segmentation, Customer Persona Integration, Onboarding Management, Adoption Management, Retention Management, Renewal Management, Customer Engagement, Customer Advocacy, Voice of Customer, Customer Feedback Collection, Customer Sentiment Analysis, Experience Analytics, AI Recommendations, Executive CX Dashboards, and Customer Experience Governance.
- **FR-007**: System MUST NOT replace CRM systems or Help Desk software, MUST NOT automatically execute customer communications without approval, MUST NOT modify customer accounts without authorization, MUST NOT replace legal or compliance review, MUST NOT publish customer information externally, and MUST NOT override customer privacy preferences.
- **FR-008**: System MUST implement a layered architecture consisting of: Layer 1 (Customer Interaction Sources — mobile app, website, community, membership portal, learning platform, support, AI chat, events, webinars, surveys, emails, push, social, referral programs); Layer 2 (Customer Data Collection — profile, device, session, navigation, purchase, learning, community activity, feedback, support history, satisfaction scores); Layer 3 (Customer Intelligence — AI classification, health scoring, frustration detection, churn/upsell prediction, next-best-action recommendation, success-signal detection, engagement-pattern identification); Layer 4 (Experience Optimization — auto-generated personalized recommendations, journey improvements, success plans, engagement campaigns, risk alerts, executive insights); and Layer 5 (Executive Intelligence — health, journey, success, churn, satisfaction, and VoC dashboards plus AI executive summary).
- **FR-009**: System MUST progress every customer relationship through a standardized 15-stage lifecycle (Visitor, Lead, Prospect, Trial User, New Customer, Onboarding, Activated User, Engaged Customer, Loyal Customer, Advocate, Renewal, Expansion, Win-Back, Alumni, Archived), and each stage MUST support configurable automation rules, KPIs, engagement strategies, and success metrics.
- **FR-010**: System MUST implement the 14-phase Customer Experience Operating Model (Customer Acquisition → Customer Qualification → Product Purchase → Customer Onboarding → Activation → Product Adoption → Customer Success → Engagement → Retention → Renewal → Expansion → Advocacy → Referral → Continuous Experience Improvement), and every phase MUST support measurable KPIs, ownership, automation rules, AI recommendations, and executive reporting.

#### Journey Mapping

- **FR-011**: System MUST connect every customer touchpoint into a unified, end-to-end journey that is measurable, personalized, AI-assisted, and continuously optimized rather than treating interactions as isolated events.
- **FR-012**: System MUST support configurable journey types including Visitor, Lead, Trial, Customer Onboarding, Membership, Product Adoption, Learning, Community, Support, Renewal, Upgrade, Referral, Re-engagement, and Win-back Journeys, and MUST allow administrators to create additional journey types without software modification.
- **FR-013**: System MUST progress each customer journey through a 9-stage lifecycle (Created, Draft, Under Review, Approved, Published, Active, Optimized, Deprecated, Archived), and MUST maintain complete version history and audit records for every journey revision.
- **FR-014**: System MUST allow each journey map to define Journey Name, Customer Persona, Lifecycle Stage, Business Objective, Customer Goal, Journey Description, Entry Point, Exit Point, Success Criteria, Owner, Status, and Review Schedule.
- **FR-015**: System MUST support configurable journey stages (Awareness, Consideration, Evaluation, Registration, Purchase, Onboarding, Activation, Product Usage, Support, Renewal, Advocacy) with the ability to configure additional stages, and each stage MUST capture Customer Goals, Customer Actions, User Interface Screens, Business Processes, Customer Emotions, Pain Points, Opportunities, Internal Teams, Supporting Systems, and Success Metrics.
- **FR-016**: System MUST support journey visualization in at least 8 modes: Timeline View, Flow Diagram, Swimlane Diagram, Persona View, Channel View, Heat Map, Sankey Flow, and Interactive Journey Canvas.
- **FR-017**: System MUST measure Journey Analytics combining behavioral, operational, financial, and customer-satisfaction metrics, including Journey Completion Rate, Drop-off Rate, Conversion Rate, Time per Stage, Engagement Rate, Satisfaction Score, Customer Effort Score, Retention Rate, Churn Rate, and Revenue Contribution, and MUST monitor behavioral signals including Screen Navigation, Session Duration, Click Paths, Feature Usage, Purchase Activity, Learning Activity, Community Activity, Search Activity, Exit Behavior, and Returning Users.
- **FR-018**: AI engine MUST be able to identify Journey Bottlenecks, Friction Points, High-Value Paths, Churn Signals, Drop-off Predictions, Behavioral Patterns, Journey Segments, and Customer Intent, surfaced through a Journey Analytics dashboard showing Active Journeys, Journey Health Score, Stage Performance, Conversion Funnel, Customer Satisfaction, Engagement Trends, Revenue Attribution, and Executive Insights.
- **FR-019**: System MUST manage every customer touchpoint (Website, Mobile App, Community, Courses, Membership Portal, AI Assistant, Email, WhatsApp, Push Notifications, SMS, Phone Support, Live Chat, Social Media, Events, Webinars, Support Tickets, Knowledge Base) with Touchpoint Name, Journey Stage, Channel, Business Objective, Customer Objective, Owner, Success Metrics, Current Status, Satisfaction Score, and Automation Rules, and MUST maintain a centralized touchpoint repository linking journeys, owners, documentation, and historical performance.
- **FR-020**: System MUST monitor touchpoint performance including Customer Interactions, Response Time, Resolution Time, Satisfaction, Abandonment Rate, Conversion Rate, Revenue Impact, and Engagement Score.
- **FR-021**: System MUST support Journey Optimization as an ongoing enterprise process, considering Customer Feedback, Product Analytics, Journey Analytics, Support Data, AI Recommendations, Market Research, Customer Interviews, Experiment Results, and Business KPIs as inputs, and MUST support optimization activities including Journey Redesign, UX Improvements, Content Optimization, Feature Recommendations, Process Simplification, Automation Improvements, Personalization Enhancements, and Communication Optimization.
- **FR-022**: AI engine MAY recommend removing friction, merging journey steps, adding automation, personalizing content, introducing new features, improving navigation, simplifying workflows, or improving conversion, and every such recommendation MUST require configurable review before implementation.
- **FR-023**: System MUST ensure every customer interaction across all supported channels (Mobile Application, Web Portal, Community Platform, Learning Platform, AI Chat Assistant, Email, WhatsApp, SMS, Push Notifications, Voice Calls, Live Events, Offline Events, Social Media, Partner Channels) contributes to a single unified customer profile, and MUST support Unified Customer Identity, Cross-Channel Session Tracking, Personalized Recommendations, a Unified Notification Center, Shared Customer Preferences, Interaction History, Cross-Channel Analytics, and Channel Performance Comparison.
- **FR-024**: System MUST give authorized teams access to Customer Profile, Purchase History, Membership Details, Learning Progress, Community Activity, Support History, Product Usage, Customer Goals, and Previous Communications regardless of the channel through which the customer is being served.
- **FR-025**: System MUST enforce Journey Governance principles (Customer-Centric Design, Consistency, Accessibility, Privacy Compliance, Security, Continuous Improvement, Data Accuracy, Executive Accountability) via configurable, role-based permissions and approval responsibilities for Chief Customer Officer, CX Director, Product Manager, CSM, UX Lead, Marketing Lead, Support Manager, Compliance Officer, Data Analyst, and Executive Sponsor roles, and MUST support Journey Approval, Journey Publication, Journey Revision, Journey Retirement, Compliance Review, Accessibility Review, and Executive Approval workflows, each maintaining immutable audit logs.
- **FR-026**: System MUST provide a Journey Intelligence Dashboard aggregating customer, operational, financial, and behavioral insights into a single executive workspace, including Customer Journey Health Score, Journey Completion Rate, Stage Conversion Funnel, Customer Satisfaction, NPS, Customer Effort Score, Average Journey Duration, Journey Drop-off Analysis, Touchpoint Performance, Omnichannel Engagement, Churn Risk Distribution, Revenue Attribution, AI Journey Recommendations, and Executive Alerts.
- **FR-027**: AI engine MUST provide Journey Health Predictions, Customer Behavior Forecasts, Conversion Predictions, Churn Forecasting, Engagement Forecasting, Revenue Opportunity Detection, Journey Optimization Recommendations, and Executive Decision Support, and all such AI insights MUST be transparent, explainable, and reviewable by authorized users.

#### Customer Health Scoring & Playbooks

- **FR-028**: System MUST proactively and continuously monitor customer progress, predict risks, recommend actions, and guide customers toward measurable success (rather than only reacting to reported problems), functioning as the operational hub for customer retention, expansion, and advocacy.
- **FR-029**: System MUST provide Customer Success Platform core components: Customer Health Management, Success Plans, Customer Success Tasks, Customer Milestones, Customer Goals, Success Playbooks, Customer Risk Management, Renewal Management, Expansion Opportunity Tracking, Customer Advocacy Management, an AI Success Assistant, and Executive Customer Success Analytics.
- **FR-030**: System MUST progress every customer through a configurable 12-stage Customer Success Lifecycle (Registered, Purchased, Onboarding, Activated, Product Adoption, Engaged, Growing, Renewing, Expanding, Advocacy, Alumni, Re-engagement), each stage carrying configurable KPIs, automation rules, milestones, and AI recommendations.
- **FR-031**: System MUST continuously evaluate a Customer Health Score using operational, behavioral, financial, and engagement data across 14 categories (Product Usage, Login Frequency, Feature Adoption, Learning Progress, Community Participation, Support History, Payment Status, Subscription Status, Customer Satisfaction, NPS, Renewal Probability, AI Engagement, Goal Achievement, Account Activity), and administrators MUST be able to configure the weighting of each category.
- **FR-032**: System MUST classify customers into one of 6 configurable health score levels (Excellent, Healthy, Stable, Attention Required, At Risk, Critical) with configurable thresholds.
- **FR-033**: AI engine MUST identify Declining Engagement, Churn Signals, Renewal Risk, Upsell Opportunities, Customer Frustration, Product Misalignment, Success Probability, and Recommended Interventions, and every such prediction MUST include a confidence score and supporting evidence.
- **FR-034**: System MUST provide a Health Monitoring Dashboard displaying Overall Health Distribution, High-Risk Customers, Health Trend Analysis, Renewal Forecast, Customer Segmentation, AI Risk Alerts, and Executive Summary.
- **FR-035**: System MUST standardize best practices for recurring customer success scenarios via Success Playbooks covering New Customer Onboarding, Activation Recovery, Product Adoption, Renewal Preparation, Upsell Opportunity, Customer Escalation, High-Risk Customer Recovery, Enterprise Account Review, Community Engagement, AI Feature Adoption, and Win-Back Campaign categories.
- **FR-036**: System MUST define each playbook with Playbook Name, Business Objective, Trigger Conditions, Recommended Actions, Responsible Team, Timeline, Communication Templates, Success Metrics, and Exit Criteria.
- **FR-037**: System MUST allow playbooks to automatically trigger based on Low Health Score, Declining Usage, Failed Onboarding, Missed Milestones, Renewal Window, Churn Prediction, Product Upgrade Opportunity, or Customer Feedback conditions, and automated execution MUST remain configurable (subject to review before customer-facing action, per platform-wide AI-assistive principle).

#### Success Plans

- **FR-038**: System MUST allow Customer Success Plans defining personalized strategies tailored to a customer's objectives, maturity, and lifecycle stage, to help them achieve business goals while maximizing value from TBT products and services.
- **FR-039**: System MUST allow each success plan to define Customer Goals, Business Objectives, Success Milestones, Action Items, an Assigned Customer Success Manager, Target Dates, KPIs, Dependencies, Risks, a Review Schedule, and Completion Status.

### Key Entities *(include if feature involves data)*

- **CX Lifecycle Stage**: One of the 15 standardized stages (Visitor → Archived) every customer relationship progresses through; carries configurable KPIs, automation rules, engagement strategy, and success metrics.
- **CX Operating Model Phase**: One of the 14 phases (Acquisition → Continuous Experience Improvement) describing how customer interactions are managed enterprise-wide; carries KPIs, ownership, automation rules, and AI recommendations.
- **Customer Journey**: A configured, named journey (e.g., Onboarding Journey, Renewal Journey) of a given type, progressing through its own 9-stage lifecycle (Created → Archived) with version history.
- **Journey Map**: The visual/structured representation of a journey — name, persona, lifecycle stage, objective, entry/exit points, success criteria, owner, and status — composed of ordered Journey Stages.
- **Journey Stage / Journey Element**: A stage within a journey map (e.g., Awareness, Consideration) and its associated customer goals, actions, emotions, pain points, opportunities, and success metrics.
- **Touchpoint**: A discrete customer interaction point (channel + journey stage + owner) tracked for performance (response time, resolution time, satisfaction, conversion, revenue impact).
- **Customer Health Score (CX variant)**: The blended, weighted score across 14 categories that classifies a customer into one of 6 health tiers, with AI-generated supporting signals and confidence scores.
- **Success Plan**: A customer-specific, template-derived plan of goals, milestones, action items, KPIs, dependencies, risks, and a review schedule, owned by an assigned CSM.
- **Success Playbook**: A standardized, trigger-condition-based response pattern (recommended actions, responsible team, timeline, communication templates, exit criteria) for a recurring customer success scenario.
- **Customer Success Lifecycle Stage**: One of the 12 stages (Registered → Re-engagement) a customer progresses through within the Customer Success Platform specifically.
- **Customer Lifecycle Stage (17-stage CLM)**: One of the 17 broader lifecycle stages (Anonymous Visitor → Alumni) with entry/exit criteria, KPIs, automation, and ownership.
- **Customer Segment**: A static or dynamically-recalculated grouping of customers by demographic, behavioral, revenue, product-usage, or lifecycle criteria, with segment-level analytics.
- **Customer Persona**: A research-based profile (demographics, goals, pain points, channel/communication preferences) used for personalization and product/CX design, subject to AI-driven refinement.
- **Loyalty Program / Loyalty Benefit**: A configured rewards structure (points, tiers, badges) and its redeemable benefits, tracked via loyalty metrics per customer.
- **Advocacy Signal / Advocate**: An AI-detected combination of signals (NPS, usage, community leadership, referral success) identifying a customer as a potential advocate, and the resulting advocate record/activities.
- **Referral**: A tracked referral record progressing through the 7-step referral workflow, linked to its source, reward model, and fraud-check status.
- **VoC Data Source**: One of up to 20 configurable channels from which customer feedback/opinion signal is collected.
- **Feedback Record**: A single customer feedback item with category, sentiment score, priority, assigned team, status, and resolution notes, progressing through the 10-step feedback workflow with immutable audit trail.
- **Survey**: A designed, distributed, and analyzed structured survey instrument (CSAT/NPS/CES/etc.) with response and completion analytics.
- **Sentiment Record**: An AI-generated sentiment classification (with emotion, intent, topic, confidence score, and evidence) derived from a piece of structured or unstructured customer feedback.
- **CX Governance Record**: A logged governance action (approval, rejection, compliance/accessibility review, AI model review) tied to a specific journey, recommendation, or experience change, with an immutable audit trail and role attribution.
