# Feature Specification: Enterprise Attribution, Incrementality Measurement & Media Mix Modeling

**Feature Branch**: `037-enterprise-attribution-mmm`

**Created**: 2026-07-22

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: User description: "Volume 14 – Part 2 – Chapter 4: Enterprise Marketing Attribution, Incrementality Measurement, Media Mix Modeling & Revenue Intelligence Platform (source: `document 1/Document 1 (36).md`) — a unified system combining customer-level attribution, privacy-safe aggregate measurement, controlled experiments, media mix modeling, revenue analytics and forecasting, positioned by the source as 'the official source of truth for marketing performance, campaign impact and revenue contribution across the TBT ecosystem.'"

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Navigate Performance Through the Five-Level Marketing Measurement Framework (Priority: P1)

Marketing leadership, campaign managers, and analysts view any campaign's or channel's performance organized into five progressive levels — Delivery, Engagement, Conversion, Revenue, and Incremental Business Value — so that "was it seen," "did people interact," "did they convert," "how much revenue resulted," and "did marketing actually cause additional value" are always answered as five distinct, clearly bounded questions rather than blended into one ambiguous number.

**Why this priority**: This framework is the organizing structure the entire chapter is built on (§7) and directly answers the platform's stated purpose questions (§1). Every other capability in this spec — attribution, incrementality, MMM, revenue intelligence, budget optimization — is a deeper mechanism serving one of these five levels. Without it, there is no shared vocabulary for what "performance" means across marketing, finance, and executives.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by pulling metrics for one campaign and confirming each of the five levels renders its own defined metric set (Level 1: impressions/reach/messages delivered/video views/ad visibility; Level 2: clicks/opens/landing-page visits/video completion/downloads/likes/comments/shares/saves; Level 3: registrations/leads/membership purchases/enrollments/orders/event registrations/renewals/referrals; Level 4: gross/net/recurring revenue/AOV/upsell/cross-sell revenue; Level 5: incremental conversions/revenue/profit/retention/LTV) without any level's figures leaking into another.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a campaign with recorded ad impressions, clicks, registrations, and revenue, **When** a marketing leader opens the campaign's measurement view, **Then** Level 1 through Level 4 metrics (delivery, engagement, conversion, revenue) are each displayed under their own labeled level, sourced only from that level's defined metric list.
2. **Given** the same campaign has an approved, completed incrementality experiment, **When** Level 5 (Incremental Business Value) is viewed, **Then** incremental conversions, incremental revenue, incremental profit, incremental retention, and incremental lifetime value are shown, distinct from and never summed into the Level 3/4 conversion or revenue totals.
3. **Given** a campaign with no completed incrementality experiment, **When** Level 5 is viewed, **Then** the system indicates incremental metrics are unavailable rather than substituting attributed or influenced revenue as if it were incremental.
4. **Given** a user asks "which channel generated the customer" versus "did the campaign create additional business value," **When** each question is answered, **Then** the system routes the first to attribution (Levels 3–4) and the second to incrementality (Level 5), matching the framework's defined purpose for each level.

### User Story 2 - Designate a Primary Attribution Model for Official Reporting, With Alternates for Analysis (Priority: P1)

An Attribution Analyst or Marketing Executive configures exactly one Primary Attribution Model as the official model used for operational and financial reporting — recording its owner, approved version, effective date, business justification, review date, and executive approval — while alternative models remain available for side-by-side analysis without ever silently overriding the official model's output.

**Why this priority**: §26 makes this an explicit governance requirement, and it directly implements Constitution Article IV (Historical Immutability): every downstream dashboard, CAC/ROAS/ROI calculation, and finance report depends on which model is "official." An ungoverned or silently-swapped primary model would invalidate every financial figure built on top of it.

**Independent Test**: Configure two attribution models (e.g., Position-Based as primary, Data-Driven as alternate) against the same conversion data; confirm official dashboards and finance reports use only the primary model's output, the alternate is viewable only in a clearly labeled comparison view, and changing the primary model requires the full governance record.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** no primary model is yet configured, **When** an authorized user designates Position-Based Attribution as primary, **Then** the system requires model owner, approved version, effective date, business justification, review date, and executive approval before activation.
2. **Given** a primary model is active and an alternate (Data-Driven) model is also configured, **When** an official/finance-facing campaign report is generated, **Then** it uses only the primary model's attributed revenue and clearly labels which model produced it.
3. **Given** a user opens the Attribution Model Comparison dashboard, **When** multiple models are compared against the same conversion data, **Then** revenue by channel, conversions by channel, credit shifts, cost efficiency, customer quality, model variance, and model confidence are shown, and the system flags when business conclusions change significantly between models.
4. **Given** a request to change the primary model from Position-Based to Data-Driven, **When** the change is submitted, **Then** it requires a new business justification and executive approval and is recorded in the model's change history rather than silently replacing the prior designation.

### User Story 3 - Identity Confidence Classification Gates Customer-Level Records From Official Reporting (Priority: P1)

Every reconstructed customer journey and cross-device or cross-channel identity match is classified into an Identity Confidence Level — Verified, High Confidence, Medium Confidence, Low Confidence, Anonymous, or Unresolved — and governance policy determines which confidence levels are eligible for official customer-level reporting, so low-confidence or unresolved matches are excluded or clearly labeled rather than silently blended into trusted totals.

**Why this priority**: §28–29 make this an explicit data-integrity gate. Without it, probabilistic or unresolved identity matches could silently inflate or distort official attribution and revenue numbers, undermining every downstream financial metric — this is a foundational control of comparable severity to attribution-model governance itself.

**Independent Test**: Feed the system touchpoints with varying identity-match strength (deterministic logged-in match, probabilistic email match, device-only match, fully anonymous); confirm each is classified into the correct confidence level and that official customer-level reports exclude or separately label records below the configured confidence threshold.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a touchpoint matched via a logged-in membership ID, **When** identity confidence is classified, **Then** it is recorded as "Verified" and is eligible for official customer-level reporting.
2. **Given** a touchpoint matched only through probabilistic device or behavioral signals with no verified identifier, **When** identity confidence is classified, **Then** it is recorded as "Low Confidence" or "Anonymous," and governance policy determines whether it is excluded from official customer-level reports.
3. **Given** a governance policy excludes Low Confidence and Anonymous records from official reporting, **When** an executive dashboard is generated, **Then** those records are omitted from the official figures but remain visible in an aggregate or exploratory view with their confidence level clearly labeled.
4. **Given** a customer journey mixes touchpoints of different confidence levels (e.g., a Verified last touch preceded by an Unresolved earlier touch), **When** the journey is displayed, **Then** each touchpoint shows its own identity confidence rather than the journey being assigned one blended confidence value.

### User Story 4 - Run an Incrementality Experiment (RCT / Geo-Holdout) and Compute Incremental CPA and Incremental ROAS (Priority: P1)

A performance marketer designs a randomized controlled trial or geographic holdout test for a campaign, defining treatment and control groups and a randomization unit that minimizes contamination; once the experiment reaches its required sample size and duration, the system reports incremental lift, confidence interval, and statistical significance, plus Incremental CPA (campaign cost ÷ incremental conversions) and Incremental ROAS (incremental revenue ÷ advertising cost), keeping these causally validated figures distinct from attributed CPA/ROAS.

**Why this priority**: §39, §48, and §49 designate incrementality as the causal ground-truth measurement — the source explicitly states Incremental ROAS "shall be treated as a stronger causal metric than attributed ROAS when a valid experiment is available." This is the mechanism preventing marketing from over-crediting itself relative to what would have happened anyway, making it P1 alongside attribution governance.

**Independent Test**: Configure a geo-holdout experiment with a defined treatment/control split for one campaign, run it to completion, and confirm the resulting registry entry reports treatment/control conversion rates, absolute and relative lift, incremental conversion count, incremental revenue, confidence interval, statistical significance, Incremental CPA, and Incremental ROAS, stored separately from the campaign's attributed CPA/ROAS.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a campaign with a configured geographic holdout (treatment cities vs. control cities), **When** the experiment reaches its planned sample size and duration, **Then** the system reports treatment conversion rate, control conversion rate, absolute lift in percentage points, relative lift, incremental conversion count, confidence interval, and statistical significance.
2. **Given** the experiment has not yet reached its minimum detectable effect sample size or planned duration, **When** a user requests results early, **Then** the system prevents a declaration of success or failure and displays the experiment as still in progress.
3. **Given** the experiment concludes with a validated incremental lift, **When** Incremental CPA and Incremental ROAS are calculated, **Then** Incremental CPA equals Campaign Cost divided by Incremental Conversions and Incremental ROAS equals Incremental Revenue divided by Advertising Cost, and both are displayed distinctly from the campaign's attributed CPA and attributed ROAS.
4. **Given** a guardrail metric such as refund rate or unsubscribe rate exceeds its configured limit during the experiment, **When** the breach is detected, **Then** the experiment may be automatically paused and flagged for review before further treatment exposure continues.

### User Story 5 - Reconcile Media Mix Modeling Against Multi-Touch Attribution Rather Than Assuming Identical Results (Priority: P2)

A data scientist or marketing leader runs the Media Mix Modeling Engine on historical time-series data (spend, impressions, revenue, seasonality, external factors) to estimate aggregate channel contribution, saturation, and carryover, then compares the MMM output against multi-touch attribution output for the same period. The system explicitly identifies channels overvalued or undervalued by attribution and brand effects not captured at customer level, and it does not assume the two methods must agree.

**Why this priority**: §59–60 make this reconciliation an explicit named capability precisely because MTA and MMM structurally measure different things — customer-level tracked journeys versus aggregate time-series causal contribution. Without reconciliation, budget decisions based on either method alone risk over- or under-investing in brand, offline, or long-tail channels that MTA cannot see.

**Independent Test**: Run MMM and MTA independently over the same historical quarter for the same channels; confirm the reconciliation view shows both outputs side by side, flags any channel where MMM-estimated contribution diverges materially from MTA-attributed revenue, and does not merge or average the two into a single number.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** historical spend, impressions, revenue, seasonality, and external-factor data for the past 12 months, **When** the MMM Engine runs, **Then** it outputs channel contribution, baseline demand, incremental revenue, marginal ROI, saturation curve, carryover effect, channel interaction, and a confidence range.
2. **Given** MMM estimates television or offline media contributed meaningfully to baseline demand while MTA recorded no attributable digital touchpoints for that channel, **When** the reconciliation view is generated, **Then** the system labels television as "undervalued by attribution" and surfaces the brand-effect gap rather than reporting television as having zero contribution.
3. **Given** a channel's MTA-attributed revenue is materially higher than its MMM-estimated contribution for the same period, **When** reconciliation runs, **Then** the system flags the channel as "overvalued by attribution" and lists tracking gaps or cross-channel interaction as possible explanations.
4. **Given** both MMM and MTA results exist for the same period, **When** a report combines them, **Then** it never adds MMM incremental revenue and MTA attributed revenue together as a single total, and the system explicitly states the two methods are not required to produce identical results.

### User Story 6 - Submit a Budget Optimization Engine Recommendation for Finance/Executive Approval Before Reallocating Spend (Priority: P2)

The Budget Optimization Engine analyzes marginal returns, saturation, and optimization constraints (total budget, minimum/maximum channel spend, contractual commitments, margin, risk tolerance, compliance restrictions) and recommends a reallocation that maximizes an approved objective such as incremental revenue. The recommendation is presented with projected outcomes through the Budget Scenario Simulator and MUST NOT change financial budgets until a human approver (marketing owner, finance owner, business unit leader, or executive) records an explicit accept decision, or an autonomous-optimization policy has been explicitly approved.

**Why this priority**: §85–89 and Constitution Article II require this human-approval gate on any AI/model-generated spend decision. This is P2 rather than P1 because it depends on incrementality (P1) and MMM (P2) outputs already existing as trustworthy inputs, but it remains essential since it is the mechanism that turns measurement into an actual budget action.

**Independent Test**: Generate a budget optimization recommendation from a known set of channel marginal-return estimates and constraints; confirm the recommended allocation respects every configured constraint, is presented via the scenario simulator with revenue/CAC/ROAS/profit/risk/confidence projections, is not applied to any live budget automatically, and is only applied after an authorized approver records an explicit accept decision (with an equally recorded reject path).

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** marginal return analysis identifies a declining-return channel and an underfunded channel, **When** the Budget Optimization Engine generates a recommendation, **Then** it proposes a reallocation that maximizes the configured objective while respecting total budget, minimum/maximum channel spend, contractual commitments, and margin/risk constraints.
2. **Given** a generated recommendation, **When** a user opens the Budget Scenario Simulator, **Then** it displays estimated revenue, incremental revenue, customers, CAC, ROAS, profit, risk, and a confidence range for the proposed allocation before any change is made.
3. **Given** a budget recommendation is presented to a Finance Owner and a Marketing Owner, **When** one approver rejects it, **Then** no budget change occurs and the rejection is recorded with a reason.
4. **Given** a recommendation is approved by all required approvers, **When** the approval is finalized, **Then** the accepted recommendation, its approvers, and the resulting budget change are recorded in the audit log, and the change takes effect only after this recorded approval — never automatically.

### User Story 7 - View the Revenue Contribution Hierarchy Without Double-Counting or Blending Revenue Tiers (Priority: P2)

Marketing, finance, and executive users view a conversion's or campaign's revenue broken into the seven-tier hierarchy — Observed, Attributed, Influenced, Incremental, Recognized, Net, and Incremental Profit — each clearly labeled and never combined into a single blended figure, with refunds, cancellations, and chargebacks flowing through to update Net and Recognized figures without altering the original Observed or Attributed record.

**Why this priority**: §65 explicitly states these values "shall not be combined without clear labeling." This hierarchy is the connective structure that keeps every other revenue requirement in this chapter (marketing-sourced, influenced, incremental revenue, ROAS/ROI formulas) internally consistent — without it, executives and finance risk double-counting the same rupee under two different revenue definitions.

**Independent Test**: Take one conversion with a known gross amount, apply a multi-touch attribution model, an incrementality-validated incremental estimate, and a subsequent partial refund; confirm the system displays all seven tiers separately, that no dashboard sums two tiers into one number without an explicit label, and that the refund updates Net/Recognized figures while the original Observed and Attributed records remain intact.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a ₹4,999 membership purchase with three eligible touchpoints, **When** the revenue contribution hierarchy is displayed, **Then** Observed Revenue (₹4,999), Attributed Revenue (per touchpoint, summing to no more than ₹4,999), Influenced Revenue, and — if a validated incrementality study exists — Incremental Revenue are each shown as separate, individually labeled figures.
2. **Given** the same purchase is later partially refunded ₹1,000, **When** the refund is processed, **Then** Net Revenue and Recognized Revenue are updated to reflect the refund while the original Observed Revenue and Attributed Revenue records for the original transaction remain unchanged and retrievable.
3. **Given** a dashboard attempts to sum Attributed Revenue and Influenced Revenue into one "total marketing revenue" figure, **When** the report renders, **Then** the system prevents this combination unless each contributing tier is explicitly labeled in the output.
4. **Given** Incremental Profit is requested for a campaign, **When** it is calculated, **Then** it is shown as its own distinct tier derived from incrementality and cost data, not interchanged with Incremental Revenue or Marketing ROI.

### User Story 8 - Attribution Finalization Lifecycle With Audited Manual Adjustments (Priority: P3)

Attribution results move through defined finalization statuses — Real Time, Preliminary, Updated, Final, Adjusted — as the attribution window closes, late or offline conversions are processed, the refund period is considered, and finance reconciliation completes. Any manual adjustment to a Final result requires a reason, supporting evidence, the requesting user's identity, a timestamp, and approval, and is recorded in an immutable audit log rather than silently overwriting the prior figure.

**Why this priority**: §36, §105, and Constitution Article IV require this as the concrete implementation of historical immutability for attribution and revenue data in this chapter. It is P3 because it governs correction and finalization of numbers the P1/P2 stories above already produce, rather than being a separate measurement capability of its own.

**Independent Test**: Generate a real-time attribution result for a new conversion, allow it to progress to Preliminary and then Final as reconciliation completes, then submit a manual adjustment (e.g., for confirmed fraud) against the Final result and confirm the adjustment requires reason/evidence/approval, produces an "Adjusted" status, and preserves the original Final figure in the audit trail.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a new conversion is received, **When** attribution is first calculated, **Then** the result is marked "Real Time" or "Preliminary" and is visibly labeled as not yet finalized on any dashboard displaying it.
2. **Given** the conversion's attribution window closes and finance reconciliation completes with no discrepancies, **When** the finalization job runs, **Then** the result's status moves to "Final."
3. **Given** a Final result is later found to require correction (for example, a duplicate conversion is identified), **When** an authorized user submits a manual adjustment, **Then** the system requires a reason, supporting evidence, the requesting user's identity, a timestamp, and approval before the status changes to "Adjusted," and the pre-adjustment Final figure remains in the audit record.
4. **Given** a dashboard displays data that has not yet reached Final status, **When** a user views it, **Then** the dashboard clearly shows the data-freshness and finalization status so the user cannot mistake preliminary figures for finalized financial results.

### Edge Cases

- What happens when Media Mix Modeling and Multi-Touch Attribution materially disagree about a channel's contribution for the same period? The system must present both figures with a reconciliation label (overvalued/undervalued/aligned) rather than silently averaging, blending, or picking one method as automatically correct.
- What happens when a Low Confidence, Anonymous, or Unresolved identity-confidence record leaks into an official customer-level report because a governance threshold was not enforced at the reporting layer rather than only at ingestion? The reporting layer itself must re-check and enforce the confidence gate, not rely solely on upstream filtering.
- What happens when a member of an incrementality experiment's control group is inadvertently exposed to the campaign — e.g., through audience overlap, a shared promotion code, or a sales team contacting them directly? The experiment must be flagged as contaminated for analysis rather than silently included in the final incrementality result.
- What happens when Media Mix Modeling shows an apparent decline in a channel's contribution that is actually a seasonality confound (e.g., a festival period or salary cycle) rather than a real change in channel effectiveness? Seasonality controls must be applied and documented before crediting or blaming a channel for the change.
- What happens when a single conversion is simultaneously claimed by an affiliate commission and by a paid-social attribution credit? Duplicate commission and attribution claims across channels must be resolved centrally so the same conversion is not double-paid or double-counted in revenue reporting.
- What happens when a membership purchase already reported to finance as "Final" is later refunded or charged back? Net Revenue, Recognized Revenue, and refunded-revenue figures must update to reflect the refund without silently altering or deleting the original Observed and Attributed revenue snapshot.
- What happens when the Primary Attribution Model is recalibrated to a new version after historical reports were already generated and consumed by finance or executives? Finalized reports must remain traceable to the model version that was actually in effect when they were calculated; the new version must not retroactively re-baseline them.
- What happens when an incrementality experiment's guardrail metric (e.g., refund rate, complaint rate, churn) breaches its configured limit mid-experiment? The platform must be able to automatically pause the experiment rather than running it to a statistically clean but commercially or ethically harmful conclusion.
- What happens when cross-device identity resolution fails for a customer who switches from mobile to desktop mid-journey, fragmenting one true journey into two partial journeys with different identity-confidence levels? The system must not silently report this as two separate customers, nor blend the fragments into one falsely "Verified" journey.
- What happens when a budget reallocation recommendation is approved by the marketing owner but the finance owner has not yet acted? The budget change must not execute on partial approval — every required approver in the configured chain must record a decision before the change takes effect.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

#### Measurement Framework

- **FR-001**: System MUST implement a five-level Marketing Measurement Framework — Delivery Measurement, Engagement Measurement, Conversion Measurement, Revenue Measurement, and Incremental Business Value — and MUST keep each level's metrics distinctly labeled and reportable on their own.
- **FR-002**: Level 1 (Delivery) MUST capture impressions, reach, messages sent, messages delivered, video views, and advertisement visibility; Level 2 (Engagement) MUST capture clicks, opens, landing-page visits, video completion, content downloads, likes, comments, shares, and saves.
- **FR-003**: Level 3 (Conversion) MUST capture registrations, leads, membership purchases, course enrollments, product orders, event registrations, renewals, and referrals; Level 4 (Revenue) MUST capture gross revenue, net revenue, recurring revenue, average order value, subscription revenue, upsell revenue, and cross-sell revenue.
- **FR-004**: Level 5 (Incremental Business Value) MUST capture incremental conversions, incremental revenue, incremental profit, incremental retention, and incremental lifetime value, and MUST be populated only from validated incrementality sources rather than attributed or influenced figures.
- **FR-005**: System MUST connect marketing activities to the defined set of measurable business outcomes — registration, membership purchase, membership renewal, course enrollment, ebook purchase, event registration, product purchase, referral, community activation, customer retention, customer lifetime value, revenue, and profit contribution — and MUST provide trustworthy, explainable measurement even when customer-level tracking is incomplete due to privacy restrictions, device changes, offline activity, or platform limitations.

#### Marketing Taxonomy, Campaign Registry & Tracking Governance

- **FR-006**: System MUST apply a centrally governed, standardized marketing taxonomy across all campaigns covering business unit, brand, product, membership plan, campaign, channel, source, medium, creative, audience, geography, language, objective, funnel stage, promotion, agency, budget owner, and cost center, and MUST validate configurable campaign naming conventions before activation, flagging invalid records for correction.
- **FR-007**: System MUST require every marketing campaign to be registered before launch with campaign ID, name, business objective, owner, target audience, channel, product, geography, language, start/end dates, planned budget, approved budget, primary and secondary KPIs, attribution window, experiment design, approval status, and tracking readiness status.
- **FR-008**: System MUST generate and manage standardized tracking parameters (source, medium, campaign, content, term, creative ID, advertisement ID, audience ID, promotion ID, affiliate ID, referral ID), allow users to generate trackable links from the campaign registry, and MUST detect missing parameters, invalid values, duplicate campaign codes, expired links, inconsistent naming, and unregistered sources.

#### Touchpoint Collection & Customer Journey Reconstruction

- **FR-009**: System MUST capture eligible marketing touchpoints (advertisement impression/click, email open/click, WhatsApp/SMS link click, push notification open, organic search visit, social interaction, affiliate referral, webinar/event attendance, sales call, community invitation, content download, landing-page visit) with touchpoint ID, customer or anonymous ID, session ID, campaign ID, channel, source, medium, creative ID, device, timestamp, geography, referrer, landing page, interaction type, cost allocation, consent status, identity confidence, data source, and processing status.
- **FR-010**: System MUST capture defined conversion events (account created, lead submitted, membership started/upgraded/renewed, course purchased/enrolled, ebook purchased, event ticket purchased, product ordered, referral completed, consultation booked, payment completed, subscription activated) with conversion ID, customer ID, conversion type, revenue, currency, product, timestamp, order ID, attribution eligibility, refund status, and cancellation status.
- **FR-011**: System MUST reconstruct the sequence of eligible touchpoints before and after a conversion into a customer journey showing touchpoint order, time between touchpoints, channel, campaign, creative, device, conversion value, attribution credit, and customer identity confidence, and MUST support configurable journey/attribution windows (e.g., 1, 7, 14, 30, 60, 90 days, or custom) independently configurable per conversion type.

#### Attribution Models & Identity Confidence

- **FR-012**: The Attribution Engine MUST assign conversion credit to eligible touchpoints using rule-based, position-based, time-based, algorithmic, custom business, channel-specific, and campaign-specific attribution, and every attribution result MUST be reproducible and linked to a specific model version.
- **FR-013**: System MUST support First-Touch attribution, giving full credit to the first eligible touchpoint, for customer acquisition source, brand discovery, top-of-funnel, and new-user origin analysis, while documenting its limitation that later supporting touchpoints are not measured.
- **FR-014**: System MUST support Last-Touch attribution, giving full credit to the final eligible touchpoint before conversion, for immediate-conversion and direct-response analysis, while documenting its limitation that it may undervalue awareness and nurturing channels.
- **FR-015**: System MUST support Last Non-Direct attribution, giving credit to the final eligible non-direct marketing interaction, and MUST prevent a direct visit from replacing an earlier known marketing source within the configured attribution window.
- **FR-016**: System MUST support Linear attribution, giving all eligible touchpoints equal conversion credit.
- **FR-017**: System MUST support Time-Decay attribution, giving touchpoints closer to conversion more credit than earlier touchpoints, with a decay rate configurable by conversion type, product, campaign objective, customer lifecycle, and journey duration.
- **FR-018**: System MUST support Position-Based attribution with configurable allocation percentages across journey positions (e.g., first touch 40%, last touch 40%, middle touchpoints 20%).
- **FR-019**: System MUST support Data-Driven Attribution using statistical or machine-learning models evaluating touchpoint presence, order, time gaps, channel combinations, creative interactions, conversion probability, customer segment, journey length, and campaign frequency, and MUST provide model confidence and explanation alongside every result.
- **FR-020**: System MUST provide an Attribution Model Comparison capability allowing authorized users to compare multiple models against the same conversion data, displaying revenue by channel, conversions by channel, credit shifts, cost efficiency, customer quality, model variance, and model confidence, and MUST clearly identify when business conclusions change significantly between models.
- **FR-021**: System MUST allow configuration of exactly one Primary Attribution Model for official operational reporting, recording model owner, approved version, effective date, business justification, review date, change history, and executive approval, and MUST prevent alternative models used for analysis from silently replacing the official model's output.
- **FR-022**: System MUST classify attribution identity into Identity Confidence Levels — Verified, High Confidence, Medium Confidence, Low Confidence, Anonymous, Unresolved — and MUST allow governance policy to exclude low-confidence records from official customer-level reporting.
- **FR-023**: System MUST attempt cross-device attribution connecting eligible activities across mobile, tablet, desktop, browser, mobile application, and shared devices using logged-in customer ID, verified email, verified phone number, membership ID, first-party device identifiers, and consent-approved identity signals, and MUST record identity confidence for every reconstructed journey.

#### Cross-Channel, Offline, Partner & Adjustment Attribution

- **FR-024**: System MUST connect touchpoints across paid social, paid search, organic search, email, WhatsApp, SMS, push, community, affiliates, events, webinars, sales, direct traffic, and offline campaigns, and MUST resolve channel duplication and overlapping conversion claims centrally.
- **FR-025**: System MUST support offline conversion imports from physical events, sales teams, call centers, bank transfers, partner stores, training centers, business consultations, and manual membership activation, each requiring a reliable matching key and source timestamp.
- **FR-026**: The affiliate module MUST track affiliate ID, referral link, click, lead, conversion, revenue, commission, refund, fraud status, and attribution window, and the platform MUST prevent duplicate commission claims across affiliates and other channels; influencer performance MUST be measured using unique links, promotion codes, landing pages, campaign identifiers, survey responses, conversion uploads, and incrementality tests, reporting reach, engagement, leads, conversions, revenue, customer quality, cost per acquisition, and incremental lift.
- **FR-027**: System MUST measure eligible organic sources (organic search, direct content discovery, organic social, community sharing, word of mouth, customer referrals, unpaid creator content) separately from paid media contribution, and MUST classify unattributed or dark traffic using available evidence (landing page, referral pattern, campaign timing, geography, device behavior, survey, promotion code, session history), clearly labeling estimated classifications as such rather than presenting them as verified fact.
- **FR-028**: System MUST support self-reported attribution via customer forms (e.g., "how did you hear about TBT") as a supplement to, and never a silent replacement for, behavioral attribution.
- **FR-029**: System MUST allow authorized users to make controlled manual attribution adjustments (for failed campaign tracking, delayed offline records, incomplete platform data, duplicate conversion data, refunds, or confirmed fraud), each requiring a reason, supporting evidence, user identity, timestamp, approval, and audit record.
- **FR-030**: System MUST update attributed revenue when orders are refunded, memberships cancelled, payments fail, chargebacks occur, or products are returned, and reports MUST support gross attributed revenue, refunded revenue, net attributed revenue, and final recognized revenue as distinct figures; revenue intelligence MUST distinguish booked, collected, deferred, recognized, refunded, tax, discount, commission, payment-fee, and net revenue, with Finance defining the official revenue field used for executive marketing reporting.

#### Incrementality Measurement

- **FR-031**: System MUST determine incrementality by comparing treatment and control groups to estimate incremental conversions, incremental revenue, incremental retention, incremental engagement, incremental profit, and incremental lifetime value.
- **FR-032**: System MUST support randomized controlled trials, customer holdout tests, geographic holdout tests, channel holdout tests, campaign holdout tests, time-based tests, matched-market tests, creative incrementality tests, offer incrementality tests, and conversion-lift studies.
- **FR-033**: System MUST maintain stable treatment-group and control-group assignments throughout the approved experiment period, with randomization occurring at a configurable unit (customer, household, device, geography, city, branch, organization, audience segment, or time period) chosen to reduce contamination between groups.
- **FR-034**: System MUST support holdout management (global, channel, campaign, segment, long-term, temporary) and MUST exclude holdout customers from the relevant marketing activation through automated suppression rules.
- **FR-035**: System MUST calculate incremental conversion results distinguishing absolute lift, relative lift, incremental conversion count, incremental revenue, confidence interval, and statistical significance.
- **FR-036**: Each experiment result MUST report sample size, treatment size, control size, baseline rate, observed lift, confidence level, confidence interval, statistical significance, minimum detectable effect, test duration, and data-quality warnings, and the system MUST prevent premature declarations of success when the required sample size or duration has not been reached.
- **FR-037**: System MUST support experiment guardrail metrics (unsubscribe rate, complaint rate, refund rate, customer support contacts, satisfaction, margin, delivery failure, brand safety incidents, churn, app uninstallation) and MUST support automatically pausing an experiment when a guardrail limit is exceeded.
- **FR-038**: System MUST detect potential experiment contamination — control users receiving campaign messages, treatment/control users sharing promotion codes, unresolved cross-device identity, overlapping campaign audiences, sales teams contacting control users, or external campaigns affecting both groups — and MUST flag contaminated experiments for analysis.
- **FR-039**: System MUST calculate Incremental Cost Per Acquisition as Campaign Cost ÷ Incremental Conversions, kept separate from attributed cost per acquisition.
- **FR-040**: System MUST calculate Incremental Return on Advertising Spend as Incremental Revenue ÷ Advertising Cost, and MUST treat Incremental ROAS as a stronger causal metric than attributed ROAS when a valid experiment is available.
- **FR-041**: System MUST store every completed incrementality study in an Incrementality Results Registry with experiment ID, campaign, channel, audience, treatment definition, control definition, start/end dates, costs, outcome metrics, incremental lift, confidence, limitations, approval, and final recommendation, and MUST make historical results reusable for planning and media mix modeling.

#### Media Mix Modeling

- **FR-042**: The Media Mix Modeling Engine MUST estimate aggregate channel contribution using historical time-series data, for use when customer-level tracking is incomplete, privacy restrictions limit direct attribution, offline media is involved, brand campaigns have long-term effects, multiple channels operate simultaneously, or executive budget allocation requires aggregate analysis.
- **FR-043**: The MMM Engine MUST accept inputs including channel spending, impressions, reach, clicks, campaign activity, revenue, conversions, price changes, promotions, product launches, seasonality, holidays, economic conditions, competitor activity, organic demand, distribution changes, events, and external disruptions, and MUST support paid search, paid social, display, video, influencer, affiliate, email, SMS, WhatsApp, push, events, sponsorship, outdoor, radio, television, print, organic content, and community programs as covered channels.
- **FR-044**: The model MUST account for carryover effects (marketing impact continuing after campaign delivery) with configurable and validated assumptions.
- **FR-045**: The model MUST account for saturation effects and diminishing returns, identifying the efficient spending range, saturation point, overspend risk, and underinvestment opportunity for each channel.
- **FR-046**: The model MUST apply and document seasonality controls (weekends, month-end periods, festival seasons, new-year periods, school/college schedules, salary cycles, product-launch periods, membership-renewal cycles, regional events) and external factor controls (economic conditions, competitor campaigns, platform outages, weather events, regulatory changes, public events, market demand, price changes, distribution availability) used in the model.
- **FR-047**: MMM outputs MUST include channel contribution, baseline demand, incremental revenue, marginal ROI, average ROI, saturation curve, carryover effect, channel interaction, forecast, recommended budget, and confidence range.
- **FR-048**: System MUST compare customer-level attribution with aggregate media mix results, identifying channels overvalued or undervalued by attribution, brand effects not captured at customer level, tracking gaps, cross-channel interaction, long-term influence, and offline contribution, and MUST NOT assume that both methods must produce identical results.
- **FR-049**: System MUST combine multi-touch attribution (customer journey visibility), incrementality testing (causal validation), media mix modeling (aggregate budget planning), self-reported attribution (customer perception), and revenue intelligence (financial validation) into a Unified Measurement Framework providing a more balanced view than any single method.

#### Revenue Contribution Hierarchy

- **FR-050**: The Revenue Intelligence Platform MUST connect marketing activities with leads, customers, orders, memberships, renewals, revenue, margin, customer lifetime value, retention, churn, and profit, allowing marketing, finance, and revenue teams to use consistent definitions.
- **FR-051**: System MUST define Marketing-Sourced Revenue via configurable qualification rules, identifying new customer revenue, new membership revenue, first-purchase revenue, first-year revenue, and lifetime revenue; Marketing-Influenced Revenue MUST include conversions where an eligible touchpoint occurred within the configured window and MUST NOT be presented as fully caused by marketing unless validated through incrementality analysis; Marketing Incremental Revenue MUST be sourced only from controlled experiments, validated causal models, media mix modeling, or approved incrementality studies.
- **FR-052**: Reports MUST distinguish the seven-tier Revenue Contribution Hierarchy — Observed Revenue, Attributed Revenue, Influenced Revenue, Incremental Revenue, Recognized Revenue, Net Revenue, and Incremental Profit — and these values MUST NOT be combined without clear labeling.
- **FR-053**: System MUST evaluate campaigns using customer-quality indicators beyond immediate conversion (membership renewal, course completion, community activity, referral rate, refund rate, support cost, retention, lifetime revenue, lifetime profit, customer satisfaction) and MUST group customers into cohorts (by acquisition month, campaign, channel, product, geography, audience, membership type, first purchase, referral source) tracking revenue and retention over time.
- **FR-054**: System MUST calculate gross revenue retention, net revenue retention, membership renewal rate, expansion revenue, contraction revenue, churned revenue, and reactivation revenue, reporting marketing influence on retention separately from acquisition.
- **FR-055**: System MUST support funnel analytics across defined stages (impression, click, landing-page visit, lead, registration, trial, purchase, renewal, advocacy) reporting stage volume, conversion rate, drop-off rate, time to next stage, cost per stage, revenue contribution, and channel performance.
- **FR-056**: For customers purchasing multiple products, system MUST track acquisition product, first purchased product, cross-sold product, upsold product, renewal product, total customer revenue, and campaign influence across products; for memberships and recurring products, system MUST distinguish initial subscription revenue, renewal revenue, upgrade revenue, downgrade impact, reactivation revenue, expansion revenue, and churned revenue, supporting both first-payment attribution and lifetime-revenue analysis.

#### Financial Formulas

- **FR-057**: System MUST capture marketing costs (advertising spend, agency fees, creative production, technology cost, influencer fees, affiliate commissions, event cost, sponsorship cost, employee allocation, vendor fees, data costs, promotion discounts, coupon cost), allocate shared costs by campaign, channel, product, business unit, geography, customer segment, revenue share, impression share, usage, or manual finance allocation with the allocation method documented and version controlled, and distinguish planned, approved, committed, invoiced, paid, accrued, actual, forecast, and remaining budget/cost.
- **FR-058**: System MUST calculate Return on Advertising Spend as Attributed or Incremental Revenue ÷ Advertising Spend, with every report specifying whether the revenue basis is attributed, influenced, incremental, gross, net, or recognized.
- **FR-059**: System MUST calculate Marketing ROI as (Marketing-Generated Profit − Marketing Cost) ÷ Marketing Cost, using finance-approved configurable cost and profit definitions.
- **FR-060**: System MUST calculate Customer Acquisition Cost as Eligible Acquisition Cost ÷ New Customers Acquired, available by channel, campaign, product, geography, segment, membership plan, creative, agency, and period, and MUST distinguish Paid CAC, Blended CAC, Incremental CAC, Fully Loaded CAC, and First-Order CAC.
- **FR-061**: System MUST calculate Customer Lifetime Value from historical revenue, predicted revenue, retention probability, purchase frequency, average order value, gross margin, subscription duration, churn probability, and servicing cost, and MUST calculate the LTV:CAC ratio as Customer Lifetime Value ÷ Customer Acquisition Cost, available by acquisition source and customer cohort.
- **FR-062**: System MUST calculate CAC Payback Period as the number of months required to recover customer acquisition cost from gross profit, measured by channel, campaign, product, membership, segment, and cohort.

#### Budget Optimization

- **FR-063**: The Forecasting Engine MUST predict leads, new customers, membership purchases, renewals, revenue, marketing costs, CAC, ROAS, LTV, and incremental revenue by day, week, month, quarter, campaign, channel, product, and geography, supporting baseline, conservative, expected, aggressive, budget-reduction, budget-increase, channel-reallocation, new-campaign, new-product-launch, and economic-slowdown scenarios; every forecast MUST display point estimate, lower range, upper range, confidence score, input assumptions, model version, data freshness, and known limitations.
- **FR-064**: The Budget Optimization Engine MUST recommend spending allocations that maximize approved objectives (revenue, incremental revenue, profit, new customers, membership growth, retention, customer lifetime value, qualified leads, brand reach).
- **FR-065**: Budget recommendations MUST respect optimization constraints including total budget, minimum channel spend, maximum channel spend, contractual commitments, geography requirements, brand investment rules, campaign capacity, audience size, inventory, margin, risk tolerance, and compliance restrictions.
- **FR-066**: System MUST estimate marginal return analysis — the additional return expected from the next unit of spending — identifying the best next investment, declining-return channels, overspent channels, underfunded channels, and budget reallocation opportunities; the Budget Scenario Simulator MUST let users modify total budget, channel allocation, campaign duration, product priority, geography, audience, and target outcome, estimating resulting revenue, incremental revenue, customers, CAC, ROAS, profit, risk, and confidence range.
- **FR-067**: AI-generated budget recommendations MUST NOT automatically change financial budgets unless autonomous optimization is explicitly approved; approval MUST be obtainable only from a marketing owner, finance owner, business unit leader, or executive approver as configured, and every accepted or rejected recommendation MUST be recorded.

#### Finalization States

- **FR-068**: Attribution results MUST remain provisional until the attribution window closes, late conversions are processed, the refund period is considered, offline records are imported, and finance reconciliation is completed; the system MUST support the finalization statuses Real Time, Preliminary, Updated, Final, and Adjusted.
- **FR-069**: Once an attribution or revenue-contribution record reaches Final status, it MUST NOT be silently altered by later configuration or model changes; any correction MUST follow the manual-adjustment process (FR-029) producing an Adjusted status while preserving the original Final figure for audit.
- **FR-070**: Every dashboard MUST display last refresh time, source freshness, processing delay, incomplete sources, estimated-data indicators, and finalized-data status, so users cannot mistake partial intraday data for finalized financial results.

#### Data Governance, Model Governance & Reconciliation

- **FR-071**: System MUST maintain a Measurement Definitions Catalog with approved definitions for conversion, lead, new customer, active customer, attributed revenue, influenced revenue, incremental revenue, CAC, ROAS, ROI, LTV, churn, retention, profit, and campaign cost, each recording business owner, formula, data source, effective date, version, and approval status.
- **FR-072**: System MUST reconcile data across advertising platforms, payment gateway, ecommerce, membership system, CRM, finance system, marketing automation, and analytics tools, identifying missing records, duplicate records, currency mismatch, time-zone mismatch, revenue mismatch, refund mismatch, spend mismatch, and conversion-count mismatch.
- **FR-073**: System MUST support transaction currency, reporting currency, exchange rate, exchange-rate date, currency conversion method, and finance-approved rate source, preserving the conversion rate used at the time of reporting in historical reports; system MUST store timestamps in a standardized format while supporting local reporting time zones, clearly identifying the time zone used and preventing campaign/conversion dates from shifting incorrectly across regions.
- **FR-074**: Each attribution, forecasting, or media mix model MUST record model ID, owner, business objective, training data, features, assumptions, validation results, bias review, version, deployment date, review date, approval status, and retirement status, and MUST undergo validation including historical back-testing, out-of-sample testing, error analysis, stability testing, sensitivity analysis, incrementality comparison, business plausibility review, data leakage review, drift monitoring, and finance review where applicable.
- **FR-075**: System MUST explain important model outputs in plain language (e.g., which inputs drove a channel's contribution change and at what confidence), and MUST monitor input-data drift, channel-mix changes, cost changes, customer-behavior changes, prediction error, revenue variance, attribution instability, and forecast bias, flagging models that exceed approved thresholds for review or retraining.

- **FR-077**: Campaign and Channel Performance Dashboards MUST display objective, budget, spend, impressions, reach, clicks, leads, conversions, attributed revenue, incremental revenue, CAC, marginal CAC, ROAS, incremental ROAS, customer quality/lifetime value, saturation risk, experiment result, forecast, and budget recommendation.
- **FR-078**: System MUST provide Creative Performance analytics comparing image, video, copy, headline, call-to-action, language, format, creator, and offer variants on engagement, conversion, revenue, incremental lift, fatigue, frequency, cost efficiency, and customer quality; Audience Performance reports MUST display size, reach, frequency, conversion, revenue, incremental lift, CAC, LTV, retention, saturation, and audience overlap.
- **FR-079**: Geographic performance reports MUST support country, state, district, city, region, and PIN-code granularity, including both performance and confidence based on available data volume; Time-based performance analysis MUST cover hour of day, day of week, week, month, quarter, season, and pre/post-campaign periods.
- **FR-080**: System MUST generate attribution alerts (missing campaign parameters, sudden unattributed traffic, duplicate conversion records, revenue mismatch, tracking interruption, platform reporting discrepancy, excessive direct traffic, identity resolution decline, attribution model failure, unusual channel credit shift) and financial alerts (budget overspend, cost spike, invoice mismatch, negative ROI, CAC threshold breach, revenue decline, forecast miss, margin reduction, unusual refund rate, low budget utilization).
- **FR-081**: The AI Marketing Measurement Assistant MUST answer questions such as why campaign revenue declined, which channel produced the best incremental return, what caused CAC to increase, where next month's budget should be invested, and which attribution model changed a result, returning an answer, supporting metrics, data period, confidence, assumptions, recommended action, and source dashboards; AI-generated narrative summaries MUST be reviewable before external distribution.

### Key Entities *(include if feature involves data)*

- **Marketing Campaign**: A registered marketing initiative with taxonomy dimensions, budget tiers (planned/approved/committed/actual), owner, KPIs, attribution window, and linked experiment design; the unit campaign performance and cost are organized around.
- **Marketing Touchpoint**: A single eligible customer/marketing interaction (impression, click, open, visit, etc.) with identity linkage, channel/campaign/creative metadata, cost allocation, consent status, and an Identity Confidence classification.
- **Customer Journey**: The reconstructed, time-ordered sequence of a customer's eligible touchpoints leading to (and following) a conversion, carrying per-touchpoint attribution credit and identity confidence.
- **Conversion Event**: A measurable business outcome (membership purchase, enrollment, referral, etc.) with revenue, currency, product, order reference, refund/cancellation status, and attribution eligibility.
- **Attribution Model**: A configured methodology (first-touch, last-touch, linear, time-decay, position-based, data-driven, etc.) with a versioned identity, owner, and reproducible calculation logic.
- **Primary Attribution Model**: The single attribution model designated for official reporting, carrying model owner, approved version, effective date, business justification, review date, change history, and executive approval; distinct from alternate models used only for analysis.
- **Identity Confidence Level**: A classification (Verified, High, Medium, Low, Anonymous, Unresolved) applied to a touchpoint or reconstructed journey, gating eligibility for official customer-level reporting.
- **Attribution Result**: A calculated credit/revenue allocation for one touchpoint against one conversion under one model version, with confidence score, finalization status, and adjustment reason if applicable.
- **Incrementality Experiment**: A configured causal test (RCT, geo-holdout, channel/campaign holdout, matched-market, etc.) with hypothesis, randomization unit, treatment/control definitions, guardrail metrics, sample size, observed lift, and approval status.
- **Treatment/Control Group Assignment**: The stable membership record placing a customer, household, device, or geography into the exposed (treatment) or withheld (control) side of an incrementality experiment for its approved duration.
- **Incrementality Results Registry Entry**: A stored, reusable record of a completed incrementality study's design, costs, outcomes, incremental lift, confidence, limitations, and final recommendation.
- **Media Mix Model (MMM)**: A time-series model estimating aggregate channel contribution, baseline demand, saturation curve, carryover effect, and marginal/average ROI from historical spend, revenue, and control-variable inputs, with its own governance record and validation history.
- **Revenue Contribution Record**: A conversion's or campaign's revenue broken into the seven labeled tiers — Observed, Attributed, Influenced, Incremental, Recognized, Net, and Incremental Profit — that must never be silently combined.
- **Marketing Cost Record**: A captured cost entry (ad spend, agency fee, influencer fee, etc.) with cost type, allocation method, planned/committed/invoiced/paid amounts, currency, cost center, and finance status.
- **Budget Scenario / Budget Optimization Recommendation**: A simulated or AI-generated proposed reallocation of spend against an approved objective and constraints, carrying projected revenue/CAC/ROAS/profit/risk/confidence and a required approval-chain record before it can take effect.
- **Attribution Finalization State**: The lifecycle status (Real Time, Preliminary, Updated, Final, Adjusted) of an attribution or revenue-contribution record, gating whether it may be presented as a finalized financial result and whether it may be modified only through the audited manual-adjustment workflow.
- **Model Governance Record**: The documented owner, training data, assumptions, validation results, bias review, version, deployment/review dates, and approval/retirement status for any attribution, forecasting, or MMM model.
- **Measurement Definition**: A catalog entry defining an approved business term (e.g., "conversion," "CAC," "incremental revenue") with owner, formula, data source, effective date, version, and approval status.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: Marketing leadership can trace any campaign's performance through all five levels of the Marketing Measurement Framework (delivery through incremental business value) in a single connected view, without consulting disconnected reports.
- **SC-002**: Exactly one Primary Attribution Model is active for official reporting at any time, and 100% of executive and finance dashboards source their attributed-revenue figures from it rather than from an alternate model.
- **SC-003**: Audits of official customer-level reports find zero records below the governance-approved Identity Confidence threshold.
- **SC-004**: 100% of completed incrementality experiments used in a budget decision report Incremental CPA, Incremental ROAS, confidence interval, and statistical significance before that decision is made.
- **SC-005**: Media Mix Modeling and Multi-Touch Attribution outputs are reconciled for every closed reporting period, with every channel showing an explicit materiality flag (overvalued/undervalued/aligned) rather than a silently blended figure.
- **SC-006**: 100% of budget reallocations recommended by the Budget Optimization Engine have a recorded approval-chain decision before the change takes effect; zero unapproved autonomous reallocations occur.
- **SC-007**: An audit of generated reports and dashboard templates finds zero instances where two Revenue Contribution Hierarchy tiers are combined into a single figure without explicit labeling.
- **SC-008**: 100% of post-finalization changes to "Final" attribution or revenue-contribution records appear in the audit log with reason, evidence, requesting user, timestamp, and approval.
- **SC-009**: Standard dashboards load in under 3 seconds and preliminary attribution completes in under 10 seconds for at least 99% of requests, consistent with the stated performance targets.
- **SC-010**: Every executive-facing dashboard displays a visible data-freshness and finalization-status indicator, verified by review finding zero dashboards presenting non-Final data without such a label.

## Assumptions

- **Relationship to features 027 (`marketing-analytics-attribution`, Vol 14 Part 1 Ch 14) and 028 (`attribution-roi-measurement`, Vol 14 Part 1 Ch 15)**: This chapter's own text (§1) states the platform "shall become the official source of truth for marketing performance, campaign impact and revenue contribution across the TBT ecosystem" — a stronger and more explicit claim than either earlier chapter makes about itself. Per the constitution's guidance on Volume 14's later, redundant enterprise chapters, this spec treats feature 037 as the enterprise-wide superset that governs which of 027's and 028's already-specified attribution logic becomes authoritative for official/financial reporting once this chapter's Primary Attribution Model designation (FR-021), Identity Confidence gate (FR-022), and Unified Measurement Framework (FR-049) are implemented. Feature 027 retains ownership of general campaign/channel/funnel event tracking and dashboards; feature 028 retains ownership of the granular per-model calculation mechanics (e.g., Markov Chain, Shapley Value) and the detailed CAC/CLV/ROAS/ROI formula derivations this chapter references but does not re-derive. [NEEDS CLARIFICATION: feature 028's own spec already asserts itself as authoritative for a finalization state machine named Preliminary → Finance Reviewed → Finalized/Locked, while this chapter defines its own, differently named finalization vocabulary (Real Time → Preliminary → Updated → Final → Adjusted, §105). The source PRD does not reconcile these two competing finalization-state vocabularies across chapters; this spec preserves Chapter 4's own five-state vocabulary as written (FR-068–FR-069) rather than silently adopting feature 028's naming, and implementation planning must decide whether one finalization state machine is implemented with two display labels or whether these remain genuinely separate workflows for different record types.]
- Since features 027 and 028 already specify a working attribution engine, touchpoint pipeline, and identity-resolution foundation, this spec assumes those upstream capabilities are reused rather than rebuilt, and treats this chapter's net-new scope as: the five-level measurement framework, Primary Attribution Model governance, Identity Confidence gating, the Incrementality Experiment Engine, the Media Mix Modeling Engine, MTA/MMM reconciliation, the Revenue Contribution Hierarchy, and the Budget Optimization Engine.
- The source does not name a specific statistical test, minimum-sample-size formula, or contamination-correction procedure for incrementality experiments — only that "premature declarations of success" must be prevented (§45) and contamination "shall be flagged for analysis" (§47).
